Statement of Introduction
Saint Elizabeths Hospital Independence and Governance Amendment Act of 2026
October 5, 2026
Today, along with Councilmembers Zachary Parker and Trayon White, Sr., I am introducing the Saint
Elizabeths Hospital Independence and Governance Amendment Act of 2026. This legislation establishes
Saint Elizabeths Hospital as an independent District agency governed by a Board of Directors while
preserving its status as the District's public psychiatric hospital. Its central purpose is to strengthen
accountability and oversight, improve clinical outcomes and patient safety, and give the Hospital the
focused leadership necessary to improve and grow.
Saint Elizabeths provides inpatient psychiatric treatment, forensic services, and competency restoration for
individuals with some of the District's most complex behavioral health needs. Persistent concerns involving
staffing, safety, clinical quality, discharge delays, and regulatory compliance demonstrate the need for a
governance structure focused specifically on the Hospital. Because forensic patients occupy most of the
Hospital's beds, the District also needs a structure capable of assessing and, where appropriate, expanding
civil-commitment capacity to meet growing need.
The bill creates a 13-member Board consisting of 11 voting members appointed by the Mayor with the
advice and consent of the Council, along with the Hospital's Chief Executive Officer and Chief Medical
Officer as nonvoting members. Members will bring clinical, hospital-management, financial, patient-rights,
workforce, labor, and lived and family experience. The Board will oversee finances, governance, workforce
matters, and institutional performance. It will appoint and evaluate the CEO, establish measurable
expectations, and require corrective action when the Hospital falls short. The CEO will remain responsible
for day-to-day operations.
The legislation also establishes a nine-member Quality, Safety, and Patient Rights Committee, with a
majority of members serving from outside the Board, to provide independent, patient-centered oversight.
The Committee will review serious incidents, restraint and seclusion, staffing, clinical services and
outcomes, grievances, regulatory findings, corrective actions, and discharge delays. The bill also requires
an accessible patient bill of rights, independent advocacy, confidential reporting, and protection against
retaliation, while preserving the federal authority of the District's protection and advocacy system.
The bill also establishes a non-lapsing Hospital Fund, independent audits and reviews, public performance
reporting, and procurement and corrective-action requirements. Transferred employees will retain their pay,
leave, benefits, seniority, collective-bargaining protections, and other accrued rights. The transfer may

58 Elizabeths Hospital.

60 Price George’s County, Maryland, Arlington County, Fairfax County, Loudoun County, and Prince
61 William County, Virginia, and any municipality located within the geographic area bounded by the
62 outer boundaries of those counties.

77 Hospital, which shall be governed by a Board of Directors established in section 103.

79 hospital and provide high-quality, safe, therapeutic, recovery-oriented, trauma-informed, and rights-
80 protecting psychiatric services in the least restrictive appropriate setting and support timely transition
81 of patients to community-based services.
82 (c) The Hospital shall:
83 (1) Operate and maintain a psychiatric hospital in the District and its programs,
84 including clinical, education, research, and training programs;

85 (2) Provide Maintain all licenses, certifications, and accreditations required for
86 operation of the Hospital, including for compliance, risk-management, and patient safety procedures,

429 Sec. 114. Fiscal and quality distress response.
430 (a) The Board shall adopt objective early-warning indicators for fiscal distress, clinical-quality
431 deterioration, workforce instability, regulatory jeopardy, and persistent discharge delay.
432 (b) If an indicator is triggered, the Board shall require the Chief Executive Officer to submit a
433 remediation plan within 30 days and shall provide enhanced monthly monitoring until the Board
434 determines that the condition is resolved.
435 (c) If a condition remains unresolved for 90 days, or immediately if the condition threatens
436 patient safety, licensure, or financial solvency, the Board shall retain independent expertise and submit
437 an enhanced corrective-action plan to the Mayor and Council.
438 (d) The Mayor may direct the City Administrator to coordinate District assistance and may
439 recommend additional statutory action to the Council.
440 Sec. 115. Ethics, legal representation, liability, and tax status.
441 (a) A Board member shall not be personally liable for an action taken in good faith within the
442 scope of official duties, except for gross negligence, willful misconduct, or a knowing violation of
443 law.
19

444 (b) The Hospital may employ a General Counsel and retain outside counsel as permitted by
445 District law.
446 (c) The District may indemnify Board members, officers, and employees for acts within the
447 scope of their official duties to the extent permitted by law, and the Hospital may obtain liability and
448 directors-and-officers insurance.
449 Sec. 116. Transition from the Department.
450 (a) Within 90 days after the effective date of this act, the Mayor shall establish a Transition
451 Committee chaired by the City Administrator and including the Director, current Chief Executive
452 Officer of the Saint Elizabeths Hospital, Chief Financial Officer, Director of the Department of
453 Human Resources, Chief Procurement Officer, labor representatives, a patient or family
454 representative, a disability-rights representative, and other necessary officials.
455 (b) Within 180 days after the effective date of this act, the Transition Committee shall submit
456 to the Mayor and Council a transition plan that identifies:
457 (1) Functions, positions, personnel, appropriations, assets, liabilities, records,
458 information systems, licenses, contracts, and grants to be transferred from the Department or another
459 District agency to the Hospital;
460 (2) Functions that are to be retained by the Department or another District agency;
461 (3) A proposed transfer date and implementation schedule;
462 (4) Workforce protections and labor-consultation procedures;
463 (5) Continuity plans for patient care, pharmacy, food, facilities, security, information
464 technology, procurement, billing, legal services, and emergency operations;
465 (6) Interagency agreements required under section 11;
466 (7) Budget and capital requirements; and
20

467 (8) Risks, dependencies, and readiness criteria.
468 (c) The transition plan may provide for a phased transfer. No clinical or operational function
469 may transfer until the City Administrator certifies to the Mayor and Council that continuity of patient
470 care, payroll, procurement, financial management, information access, and regulatory compliance is
471 reasonably assured.
472 (d) The transfer date shall be October 1, 2028, or a later date established by act of the Council;
473 provided that, at least 60 days before that date, the City Administrator and Chairperson of the Board
474 shall jointly certify to the Mayor and Council that the Hospital is prepared to assume operational
475 responsibility without a material interruption in patient care, employee compensation, or regulatory
476 compliance.
477 (e) On the transfer date, the functions and associated positions, personnel, records, contracts,
478 assets, liabilities, and unexpended balances identified in the approved transition plan shall transfer to
479 the Hospital by operation of law.
480 (f) Existing licenses, certifications, privileges, contracts, orders, rules, and delegations relating
481 to transferred functions shall remain in effect until lawfully amended, replaced, expired, or revoked.
482 (g) The Hospital shall be the successor in interest for transferred contracts and obligations,
483 except as the transition plan or an act of the Council expressly provides otherwise.
484 (h) Every employee assigned to the current Saint Elizabeths Hospital and identified in the
485 transition plan shall be transferred from the Department to the Hospital without a break in service and
486 with the same base pay, accrued leave, retirement credit, health and other benefits, seniority, and other
487 rights and obligations that they had as employees of the Department before the transfer date.
488 (i) The Hospital shall assume and be bound by collective-bargaining agreements and personnel
489 contracts applicable to transferred employees, subject to their terms and applicable law.
21

490 (j) A hospital-specific personnel system shall include due process, merit-based hiring and
491 advancement, whistleblower and anti-retaliation protection, equal-employment requirements, ethics
492 and conflict rules, and preservation of accrued employee rights.
493 (k) The Hospital shall give a hiring preference to qualified District residents and shall maintain
494 workforce-development, apprenticeship, and succession plans.
495 (l) The Board shall receive quarterly workforce dashboards and shall approve an annual
496 staffing and retention plan.
497 (m) The Mayor shall submit conforming legislation to the Council no later than 270 days after
498 the effective date of this act to revise provisions of District law that assign transferred Hospital
499 functions to the Department.
