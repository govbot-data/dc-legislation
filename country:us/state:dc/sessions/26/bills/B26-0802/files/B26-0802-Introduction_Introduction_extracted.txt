Dear Secretary Howard,
Today, I am introducing the “Pied-à-Terre Property Tax Amendment Act of 2026.” This legislation
establishes a new Class 1C Property tax category on residential properties with not more than two
dwelling units, an assessed value greater than $2.558 million, and an owner that does not qualify
for the homestead deduction (i.e., non-owner-occupied housing). The real property tax rate for
Class 1C properties is set at $2.50 per $100 of assessed value.
This bill is targeted at wealthy homeowners who benefit from District infrastructure, services, and
amenities while not paying their fair share in taxes, including income taxes. Across the city, there
are homeowners who are careful to live in the District for one day less than the 183-day statutory-
residency test1 and instead establish residency in a low- or no-tax state. These owners benefit
from everything provided by the District for half of the year, but they pay no income tax. This tax
avoidance strategy increases the burden on District residents. The 1C property tax rate of $2.50
per $100 of assessed value equates to half the vacant property tax rate, reflecting the reality that
these homes are effectively vacant at least half the year.
The bill exempts properties that are undergoing construction, have a long-term tenant paying
market rent, are owned by federal appointees or members of Congress, or are actively for sale or
rent at market rates for a period of not more than nine months. Over the four-year financial plan,
this legislation is expected to raise more than $130 million2 to support District priorities, including
homeless services, education, health care, and public safety, to name only a few.
The increased property tax rate would affect a small number of housing units. In fact, of the
371,606 housing units3 in the District of Columbia, the Class 1C designation would apply to roughly
600 properties, according to the Office of Revenue Analysis. This legislation narrowly targets
wealthy owners of expensive second homes who do not maintain residence in the District.

The District faces a difficult combination of macroeconomic forces that are driving the need for
additional tax revenue. According to the most recent estimate by the Chief Financial Officer, the
District has lost 47,000 jobs year over year, while federal employment in the District has declined
by 28,000 jobs since January 2025. The unemployment rate and the office vacancy rate sit at 6.1
percent and 18.5 percent, respectively. The twelve-month average number of building permits,
which serves as a rough gauge of overall economic activity, was down 23 percent from a year
earlier. At the same time, rising prices are increasing the District’s cost of delivering services and
repairing and expanding infrastructure. Most recently, the Personal Consumption Expenditures
(PCE) price index, which is a key inflation measure, was 4.1 percent—substantially outpacing tax
receipts.4
Establishing a new Class 1C Property tax category will generate needed tax revenue without
burdening District residents and help ensure those who pursue a tax avoidance strategy pay their
fair share. This proposal is a targeted and equitable reform to the District tax code.
Should you have any questions about this legislation, please contact my Legislative Director, Kevin
DeGood, at kdegood@dccouncil.gov or (202) 615-6160.
