17 To declare the existence of an emergency with respect to the need to amend the Procurement
18 Practices Reform Act of 2010 to require the Office of Contracting and Procurement to
19 process requests for the equitable adjustment of homeless services contracts within 45
20 days.
21
22 RESOLVED BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this
23 resolution may be cited as the “Homeless Services Contract Adjustment Emergency Declaration
24 Resolution of 2026”.
25 Sec. 2. (a) The Service Contract Act (SCA) (codified at 41 U.S.C. § 351 et seq.) requires
26 District contractors and subcontractors to provide service employees like security guards, cooks,
27 and janitors with the wages and fringe benefits the Department of Labor (“Labor”) finds to be
28 prevailing in the District. A clause requiring compliance with the SCA is a standard provision in
29 District contracts, including agreements to provide homeless services like emergency shelter.
30 (b) Following oversight by the Committee on Human Services, the Executive found the
31 fringe benefits of at least one homeless services subcontractor to be out of compliance with the
32 SCA. The Department of Human Services (DHS) subsequently reminded homeless services
33 providers of the SCA payment obligations in their contracts. Several providers contend this
1

34 communication was the first indication from the Executive that SCA compliance was required and
35 that the SCA had not been discussed during the procurement process.
36 (c) After DHS’s reminder, some providers of homeless services found their payment
37 practices, and the payment practices of their subcontractors, to be inconsistent with the SCA. The
38 costs of compliance have surprised providers and threatened some organizations’ financial
39 integrity. Potential system-wide impacts are significant. For example, a recent survey of affected
40 providers found that full compliance with the SCA may impose, in calendar year 2026 alone, more
41 than $3.9 million in costs above previously budgeted wage and benefit levels. Some providers
42 describe the discovery of the costs imposed by the SCA as an emergency that has led to reductions
43 in services, including less robust shelter security and less frequent cleanings of common areas. The
44 Committee on Human Services has received significant outreach from providers and clients on the
45 impact of increased awareness of the SCA, including photographs of unsanitary shelter conditions
46 and the elimination of already strained case management staff.
47 (d) Although the District cannot excuse compliance with the SCA, providers may, through
48 the Office of Contracting and Procurement (OCP), seek equitable adjustments of their contracts to
49 account for changes in the prevailing wage determinations issued by Labor. Because the required
50 wage and benefit rates have increased so quickly, the equitable adjustment process could offer
51 providers significant relief. Family shelter contracts recently submitted to the Council required
52 compliance with a wage determination revision dated May 14, 2026. Since that revision, the
53 governing wage determination has increased twice. In only three months, the health and welfare
54 rate that covers employees’ fringe benefits increased 6.7 percent, from $962.00 a month to
55 $1,026.13 a month. The minimum hourly wage for a janitor increased from $17.84 to $18.19 over
56 that same period. Between August and May, then, the annual cost of each full-time janitor
2

57 increased by $1,497.56: $728.00 in salary and $769.56 in fringe. The equitable adjustment process
58 may account for these costs if providers pursue it.
59 (e) To avoid further reducing staff and services, and thereby compromising the health and
60 safety of unhoused residents, providers need immediate financial relief. Emergency legislation is
61 necessary to ensure they can secure any equitable adjustments to which they may be entitled
62 quickly and with the certainty of a guaranteed timeline.
63 Sec. 3. The Council of the District of Columbia determines that the circumstances
64 enumerated in section 2 constitute emergency circumstances making it necessary that the
65 Homeless Services Contract Adjustment Emergency Amendment Act should be adopted after a
66 single reading.
67 Sec. 4. This resolution shall take effect immediately.
