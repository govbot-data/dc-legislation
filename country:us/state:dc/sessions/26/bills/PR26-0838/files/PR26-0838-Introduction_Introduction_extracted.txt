12 To declare the existence of an emergency with respect to the need to amend Title II of the District
13 of Columbia Comprehensive Bicycle Transportation and Safety Act of 1984 to require that
14 electric bicycles, electric mobility devices, and traction batteries manufactured, distributed,
15 sold, or offered for lease or rent in the District meet certain safety standards; to amend the
16 District of Columbia Traffic Act, 1925 to make conforming amendments; to amend the
17 Department of Transportation Establishment Act of 2002 to authorize the Director of the
18 District Department of Transportation to develop policies for the safe use of electric
19 bicycles, motorized bicycles, and electric mobility devices; and for other purposes.
20
21
22 RESOLVED, BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this
23 resolution may be cited as the “Micromobility Fire Safety Standards Emergency Declaration
24 Resolution of 2026”.
25 Sec. 2. (a) Electric micromobility devices, such as electric bicycles (“e-bikes”) and electric
26 scooters (“e-scooters), have become increasingly common in the District. According to the
27 National Association of City Transportation Officials, the District is the largest market in the
28 country for dockless e-bikes and the second largest for dockless e-scooters. Demand is growing
29 and ridership in the first half of 2026 was 50% higher than the same period last year.
30 (b) As demand for electric micromobility devices has risen, manufacturers around the
31 world have responded by supplying the market with a wide array of options. Unfortunately, not all
32 products are manufactured at a high level of quality, which has resulted in serious safety issues.
33 The most dangerous of these product failures involve the lithium-ion batteries that power
34 micromobility devices. These batteries can experience a phenomenon called “thermal runaway,”

35 which can result in smoke, fire, the emission of harmful gasses, and even explosions. These
36 incidents typically involve batteries or other electronic components that have not been tested to
37 meet applicable safety standards.
38 (c) Requiring that micromobility devices sold or rented in the District comply with
39 applicable safety standards is a simple step to protect residents and visitors from the potential
40 dangers of electric micromobility devices.
41 (d) In response, on September 22, 2026, the Council passed the Micromobility Fire Safety
42 Standards Act of 2026 (A26-420; 73 DCR 13450). The act will reduce the fire-related safety risks
43 associated with electronic micromobility devices by requiring that any electric bicycles, electric
44 mobility devices, or traction batteries manufactured, distributed, sold, leased, or rented in the
45 District be certified to meet applicable safety standards. The act is now undergoing congressional
46 review, with a projected law date of December 19, 2026. But since implementation of these safety
47 standards presents no cost to the District, enacting identical provisions on an emergency basis can
48 provide immediate safety benefits to District residents.
49 (e) Given the present safety risks presented by products that do not comply with applicable
50 safety standards, there is an immediate need require that micromobility devices comply with
51 applicable safety standards.
52 Sec. 3. The Council of the District of Columbia determines that the circumstances
53 enumerated in section 2 constitute emergency circumstances making it necessary that the
54 Micromobility Fire Safety Standards Emergency Amendment Act of 2026 be adopted after a
55 single reading.
56 Sec. 4. This resolution shall take effect immediately.
