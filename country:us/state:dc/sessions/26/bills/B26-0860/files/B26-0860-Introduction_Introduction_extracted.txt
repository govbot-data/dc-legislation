Statement of Introduction
Responsible Implementation of Settlement Education Amendment Act of 2026
October 14, 2026
Today, I am proud to introduce the Responsible Implementation of Settlement Education
Amendment Act of 2026, along with Councilmembers Brooke Pinto, Anita Bonds, and Brianne
K. Nadeau. This bill would require a court appointed guardian managing a minor’s
settlement money to ensure the young person receives basic financial education before
they turn 18 and the guardianship terminates. The bill also requires the guardian
coordinate at least one referral to a financial counselor and provide a digestible
summary of the minor’s accounting before the remainder of their settlement funds are
turned over.
Each year, approximately 25 young people under 18 years old receive settlements or judgments
that require the Superior Court of the District of Columbia to appoint a guardian for managing
their estate. In many cases, every financial decision concerning the minor’s estate is made on
their behalf for the entirety of their childhood. The guardian distributes funds and manages all
assets by filing regular accountings, expenditures, and asset growth with the court. Once the
minor turns 18, the guardianship terminates. However, current law fails to impose a framework to
support minors' development of sustainable financial management practices without guardianship
assistance. This exacerbates risks of financial instability and mismanagement in their adult years.
This bill would impose a transition duty in the guardian's existing final-accounting obligations.
One year before the minor turns 18, the guardian would be required to arrange and document, as
part of the final account the court already reviews before authorizing turnover: (1) completion of
an approved financial literacy course; (2) a referral and initial contact with a financial counselor;
and (3) a plain-language summary of the assets and disbursement the minor would receive. This
bill would also define financial literacy course, as applied to this section, and designate the
Department of Insurance, Securities and Banking to provide or approve sufficient courses.
Assigning a guardianship over a minor’s estate inherently suggests the child needs
additional support to manage their finances. This bill aims to minimize risks of future
financial mismanagement by proactively educating young people and preparing them with tools
and resources to responsibly manage their estate throughout their adulthood. I look forward to
working with my colleagues to advance this legislation.

19 To amend section 21-120 of the District of Columbia Official Code to require minors who receive
20 settlements or inheritances over $3,000 to complete a financial literacy course before
21 reaching the age of majority.
22
23 BE IT ENACTED BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this act
24 may be cited as the “Responsible Implementation of Settlement Education Amendment Act of
25 2026”.
26 Sec. 2. Section 21-120 of the District of Columbia Official Code is amended to read as
27 follows:
28 “§ 21-120. Settlement of actions involving minor children; appointment of guardian of
29 estate.
30 “(a) A person entitled to maintain or defend an action on behalf of a minor child, including
31 an action relating to real estate, is competent to settle an action so brought and, upon settlement
32 thereof or upon satisfaction of a judgment obtained therein, is competent to give a full acquittance

33 and release of all liability in connection with the action, but such a settlement is not valid unless
34 approved by a judge of the court in which the action is pending.
35 “(b) To receive money or other property on behalf of a minor in settlement of an action, a
36 person shall be appointed as guardian of the estate of the minor, except as otherwise provided in §
37 21-2403.01, if the net value of the money and property due to the minor as a result of the
38 underlying settlement action exceeds $3,000. A guardian of the estate of a minor who receives a
39 settlement pursuant to this section shall, no later than 12 months before the minor attains the age of
40 majority, ensure that the minor:
41 “(1) Completes a financial literacy course offered or approved by the Department of
42 Insurance, Securities and Banking;
43 “(2) Is referred to, and has at least one documented contact with, a certified financial
44 counselor; and
45 “(3) Receives a written, plain-language summary of the minor’s assets held by the
46 guardian and the disbursement anticipated upon termination of the guardianship.
47 “(c)(1) The guardian shall file documentation of compliance with subsection (b) of this
48 section as part of the final account submitted to the court prior to termination of the guardianship
49 provided in § 21-143; except, that compliance with subsection (b) shall not be a condition of the
50 minor’s entitlement to receive the assets of the guardianship estate; except further, that the court
51 may withhold up to 2% of any commission compensation for services rendered by the guardian
52 until the documentation required by this subsection has been filed.
53 “(2) Notwithstanding paragraph (1) of this subsection, the court may waive any
54 requirement of subsection (b) of this section for good cause shown, including that the minor cannot
55 be located.
2

56 “(3) For purposes of this section, the term “financial literacy course” means
57 instruction in, at minimum, budgeting, saving, consumer credit, and the identification of predatory
58 financial products”.”
