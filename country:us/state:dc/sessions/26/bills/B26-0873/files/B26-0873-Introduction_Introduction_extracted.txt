Statement of Introduction
Dental Hygienist Licensing Amendment Act of 2026
October 19, 2026
Today, I am pleased to introduce the Dental Hygienist Licensing Amendment Act of
2026, along with Councilmembers Brook Pinto, Elissa Silverman, Matthew Frumin, and
Brianne Nadeau. This legislation allows internationally educated dentists to receive a license to
practice dental hygiene in the District. Furthermore, the bill grants the Mayor the authority to
determine whether the country of jurisdiction’s licensure requirements conform with the training
requirements to practice dental hygiene in the District.
Internationally trained dentists often have advanced training and experience that exceed
the training requirements for dental hygienists. Despite this, there is no existing pathway to
licensure for Internationally trained dentists to practice dental hygiene in the District. Other
jurisdictions, including Virginia and Massachusetts, have already passed legislation to allow
internationally trained dentists to practice as dental hygienists and ensure that dental practices can
meet the growing demand for oral health services.
Dental hygienists play a critical role in oral health services. They provide preventative care,
educate patients, and serve as a primary point of contact for many patients. However, the U.S. is
currently facing a major dental hygienist shortage. According to a recent survey from the American
Dental Association Health Policy Institute (HPI), only 60% of dentists reported having an adequate
number of dental hygienists on staff.1 Similarly, 91% of dentists who were actively or had recently
recruited a dental hygienist reported that recruiting was very or extremely challenging.2 In the
District, the shortage is even more acute: in 2025, the District had 320 dental hygienists compared
to 340 dentists, despite many dental offices needing more than 1 hygienist on staff for each dentist.3
As the availability of dental hygienists is a key factor in the ability of dentists to see more patients,
this ratio often results in delayed patient care and less access to preventative oral health care, which
can increase oral health related emergency department visits. The District currently has two
designated dental Health Professional Shortage Areas (HSPA) in wards 7 and 8 respectively.4
These wards also have some of the highest dental Emergency Department (ED) visits in the
District.5

23 To amend the District of Columbia Health Occupations Revision Act of 1985 to allow the Board
24 of Dentistry to grant individuals licensed to practice dentistry in a country or jurisdiction
25 outside of the United States licenses to practice dental hygiene in the District of Columbia.
26
27 BE IT ENACTED BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this
28 act may be cited as the “Dental Hygienist Licensing Amendment Act of 2026”.
29 Sec. 2. Section 504(c) of the District of Columbia Health Occupations Revision Act of
30 1985, effective March 25, 1985 (D.C. Law 6-99; D.C. Official Code § 3-1205.04(c)), is amended
31 to read as follows:
32 “(c) An individual applying for a license to practice dental hygiene under this act shall
33 establish to the satisfaction of the Board of Dentistry that the individual is:
34 “(1) A graduate of an educational program in the practice of dental hygiene of at
35 least 2 academic years which is approved by the Board; or
36 “(2) Licensed to practice dentistry in a jurisdiction outside the United States;
1

37 provided, that the Mayor shall have the authority to determine whether the country of
38 jurisdiction’s licensure requirements conform with the training requirements to practice dental
39 hygiene in the District.”.
