ROBERT C. WHITE, JR., CHAIR
COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA
Statement of Introduction:
Vacated Rates Clarification Amendment Act of 2026
_________
September 28, 2026
Today, I am introducing the Vacated Rates Clarification Amendment Act of 2026. As electricity
costs continue to climb, keeping the lights on has become difficult for many families. District
residents deserve, at minimum, certainty that the utility rates they pay are lawful, transparent,
and fairly established.
In March 2026, the D.C. Court of Appeals vacated the Public Service Commission’s approval of
Pepco’s multiyear rate plan and sent the matter back for further proceedings. However, the
current rates remain in place for now, despite the court ruling them unlawfully established. This
decision directly impacts consumers, as the overturned rate plan boosted Pepco’s authorized
revenue by $123.4 million – adding roughly $11.34 per month to the average residential bill over
2025 and 2026.
The Vacated Rates Clarification Amendment Act of 2026 provides a clear rule for what happens
when a multiyear rate plan is vacated by a court: rates would automatically revert to the most
recent lawfully approved rates until a new plan is approved so that residents are not left paying
rates without a clear, lawful basis.
This bill is a straightforward measure to provide greater certainty, transparency, and fairness for
District utility customers.

20 To amend the Public Utilities Act of 1989 to clarify that utility rates vacated by a Court,
21 regardless of whether that decision is on procedural or legal grounds, are rendered
22 unenforceable and rates automatically revert back to the most recently lawfully approved
23 rates.
24 BE IT ENACTED BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this
25 act may be cited as the "Vacated Rates Clarification Amendment Act of 2026".
26 Sec 2. Paragraph 67 of section 8 of Chapter 150 of An Act Making appropriations to
27 provide for the expenses of the District of Columbia for the fiscal year ending June thirtieth,
28 nineteen hundred and fourteen, and for other purposes, approved March 4, 1913 (37 Stat. 977;
29 D.C. Code §34-607), is amended to read as follows:
30 "(a)(1) All orders and decisions of the Commission shall remain in full effect, except as
31 provided in § 34-604 hereof, unless and until they are suspended, superseded, or rescinded by the
32 Commission or are vacated by lawful order of the District of Columbia Court of Appeals. Any
33 order or decision of the Commission establishing or approving utility rates that is vacated by the

34 Court, whether on procedural, legal, or any other grounds, shall be rendered null, void, and
35 unenforceable as of the date of such vacatur. Upon such vacatur, the applicable rates shall
36 automatically revert, without further action by the Commission, to the most recently lawfully
37 approved rates in effect prior to the vacated order or decision.
38 (2) If, in any petition made to the said Court appealing from an order or decision
39 of the Commission it be alleged that substantial and irreparable property loss would be
40 occasioned to the petitioner by the operation of the said order pending the determination of the
41 said appeal, the Court shall set a time and place for hearing upon the said allegation after not less
42 than 3 days notice to the Commission (during which period the execution of the order or decision
43 shall be stayed), and the said Court may then, upon a clear showing of the irreparable and
44 substantial property loss as alleged, suspend the effective date of the said order. No such
45 suspension shall be for a greater period than 60 days without further order after notice or hearing
46 by the Court. In the event of the issuance of an order suspending the operation of any order of the
47 Commission, the Court may include therein such provision as it deems advisable for the
48 preservation of records or accounts and the impounding or otherwise securing of moneys
49 necessary to give effect to the order of the Commission in the event the said order is sustained.
