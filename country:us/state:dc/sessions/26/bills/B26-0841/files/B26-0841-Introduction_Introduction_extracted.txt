Statement of Introduction
Business Improvement District Modernization Amendment Act of 2026
September 29, 2026
Nyasha Howard, Secretary
Council of the District of Columbia
1350 Pennsylvania Avenue, N.W.
Washington, DC 20004
Dear Secretary Howard,
Today, I am introducing the “Business Improvement District Modernization Act of 2026”. This
legislation updates and strengthens the framework governing the District’s Business
Improvement Districts (BIDs). BIDs serve as critical drivers of neighborhood vitality,
commercial attraction, and public realm management. This legislation introduces several key
updates that strengthen BIDs ability to serve their neighborhoods, support long-term investment,
and build public confidence through greater transparency and community participation:
Our BIDs have evolved significantly since the original framework was established three decades
ago. This bill reimagines the ecosystem in which BIDS operates by providing BIDs with
flexibility and stability to plan, respond to changing neighborhood needs, and invest in thriving
commercial corridors. It also makes their work more visible to the residents and businesses they
serve by providing clear, accessible information about how BIDs support local businesses,
strengthen our economy, and improve the quality of life in our communities.
To advance these goals, the Business Improvement District Modernization Amendment Act of
2026 introduces several key updates:
• Extends Renewal Terms: Extends successive BID renewal terms from 5 years to 10
years after an initial extension. This gives established BIDs long-term operational
stability, so as to not be burdened with administrative requirements;
• Standardizes Rate Adjustments: Standardizes the authority of a BID to adjust its rates
once annually by a percentage not to exceed 5 percent. This standard replaces the many
individual caps which are currently in place, and are removed by this legislation;

• Creates a Public Performance Dashboard: Requires the Department of Small and
Local Business Development (DSLBD) to establish an interactive public performance
dashboard. This tool will track core performance categories—including public safety,
cleanliness, business vitality, and small business support.
• Expanded Residential Property Collection: Refines how certain Business
Improvement Districts (BIDs) can collect revenue from residential properties they did not
before and includes reduced rates for properties with affordable housing.
• Broaden Participation in Board Governance: Requires board representation from a
resident and a Certified Business Enterprise. This expansion would bring additional
community and small business perspective into BID decisions.
It is, all told, a more transparent, standardized, and modern framework for how BIDs work
across the District. By creating a more transparent, standardized, and modern framework, this
legislation ensures our commercial corridors remain competitive, inclusive, and well-maintained.
I look forward to continuing conversations with my colleagues on the Council, the BID Council,
and community stakeholders to advance these necessary reforms.
Sincerely,
Wendell Felder
