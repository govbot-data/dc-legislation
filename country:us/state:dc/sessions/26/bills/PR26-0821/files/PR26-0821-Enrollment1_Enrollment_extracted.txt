ENROLLED ORIGINAL
A RESOLUTION
26-524
IN THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA
October 6, 2026
To declare the existence of an emergency with respect to the need to amend the Procurement
Practices Reform Act of 2010 to require the Office of Contracting and Procurement to
process requests for the equitable adjustment of homeless services contracts within 60
days.
RESOLVED, BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this
resolution may be cited as the “Homeless Services Contract Adjustment Emergency Declaration
Resolution of 2026”.
Sec. 2. (a) The McNamara-O’Hara Service Contract Act of 1965 (“SCA”), codified at 41
U.S.C. § 6701 et seq., requires District contractors and subcontractors to provide service
employees, such as security guards, cooks, and janitors with the wages and fringe benefits the
Department of Labor (“Labor”) finds to be prevailing in the District. A clause requiring
compliance with the SCA is a standard provision in District contracts, including agreements to
provide homeless services like emergency shelter.
(b) Following oversight by the Committee on Human Services, the Executive found the
fringe benefits of at least one homeless services subcontractor to be out of compliance with the
SCA. The Department of Human Services (“DHS”) subsequently reminded homeless services
providers of the SCA payment obligations in their contracts. Several providers contend this
communication was the first indication from the Executive that SCA compliance was required,
and that the SCA had not been discussed during the procurement process.
(c) After DHS’s reminder, some providers of homeless services found their payment
practices, and the payment practices of their subcontractors, to be inconsistent with the SCA. The
costs of compliance have surprised providers and threatened some organizations’ financial
integrity. Potential system-wide impacts are significant. For example, a recent survey of affected
providers found that full compliance with the SCA may impose, in calendar year 2026 alone,
more than $3.9 million in costs above previously budgeted wage and benefit levels. Some
providers describe the discovery of the costs imposed by the SCA as an emergency that has led
to reductions in services, including less robust shelter security and less frequent cleanings of
common areas. The Committee on Human Services has received significant outreach from
providers and clients on the impact of increased awareness of the SCA, including photographs of
unsanitary shelter conditions and the elimination of already strained case management staff.

(d) Although the District cannot excuse compliance with the SCA, providers may,
through the Office of Contracting and Procurement, seek equitable adjustments of their contracts
to account for changes in the prevailing wage determinations issued by Labor. Because the
required wage and benefit rates have increased so quickly, the equitable adjustment process
could offer providers significant relief. Family shelter contracts recently submitted to the Council
required compliance with a wage determination revision dated May 14, 2026. Since that revision,
the governing wage determination has increased twice. In only 3 months, the health and welfare
rate that covers employees’ fringe benefits increased 6.7%, from $962.00 a month to $1,026.13 a
month. The minimum hourly wage for a janitor increased from $17.84 to $18.19 over that same
period. Between August and May, then, the annual cost of each full-time janitor increased by
$1,497.56: $728.00 in salary and $769.56 in fringe. The equitable adjustment process may
account for these costs if providers pursue it.
(e) To avoid further reducing staff and services and thereby compromising the health and
safety of unhoused residents, providers need immediate financial relief. Emergency legislation is
necessary to ensure they can secure any equitable adjustments to which they may be entitled
quickly and with the certainty of a guaranteed timeline.
Sec. 3. The Council of the District of Columbia determines that the circumstances
enumerated in section 2 constitute emergency circumstances making it necessary that the
Homeless Services Contract Adjustment Emergency Amendment Act be adopted after a single
reading.
Sec. 4. This resolution shall take effect immediately.
