Secretary Howard,
Today I am introducing the “For-Hire Passenger Service Reform Act of 2026”, a comprehensive
package that modernizes the District’s laws governing for-hire vehicles in order to protect consumers
and the public, streamline regulations, and ensure that the District has strong tools to investigate and
enforce unlicensed and illegal operators. This text was developed based on lessons learned from the
Committee’s oversight roundtable on “The State of the For-Hire Vehicle Industry” held on May 26 and
June 3, 2026, and after an extensive review of comparable for-hire statutes and regulations in more than
20 other jurisdictions, including peer cities and states as well as international best practices.
Introducing this legislation today advances recommendations approved by the Committee on
Public Works and Operations on September 16, 2026, when the Committee adopted the “Preliminary
Report on Unauthorized Private For-Hire Vehicle Investigation”. This investigation by the Committee
has found that the District’s current for-hire vehicle laws contain extensive vulnerabilities for
exploitation by bad-faith actors. The report as adopted by the Committee recommends nothing less than
a comprehensive update of relevant laws, including the following priorities:
• Ensuring that all for-hire vehicle activity is covered by the law, even if some or all of a trip in the
District occurs without direct monetary compensation;
• Ensuring that for-hire vehicle companies cannot offload their obligations to ensure safe operation
by shifting the regulatory burden of supervising driver activity to the District, in areas that the
District cannot easily supervise;
1

• Leveling the playing field in the industry by right sizing the regulatory burden and barriers to
entry for operators;
• Establishing definitions based on types of service and without multiple co-dependencies.
• Ensuring that autonomous vehicles providing for-hire trips are covered by the FHV Law and
subject to the same service standards as any comparable for-hire trip; and
• Providing dignified and equitable access to WAV service.
Included in this proposal are updates to the District’s for-hire laws that align with international
best practices – addressing criticisms that have been identified by public witnesses, industry experts, and
agency partners over the course of many years. These reforms will break down barriers in the District’s
highly unequal and divided for-hire vehicle market, more fairly apply rules across the industry, remove
outdated requirements, bolster consumer protections and price transparency, and create more safeguards
against discrimination. In particular, the bill includes provisions to make for-hire service more
accessible, create a clearer and more enforceable “level of service” standard for wheelchair-accessible
vehicles, and establish a dedicated Accessible Passenger Service Fund.
In advance of a hearing, the Committee will provide the public and industry stakeholders with
further background materials that summarize the provisions of this legislation.
NOTE: This legislation does not amend or include any new policies or regulations related to carriers-for-hire
(food and parcel delivery). While conforming amendments may be needed to ensure consistency in terms used,
any changes to the law regarding carriers-for-hire are outside the scope of this bill.

1317 underserved areas, based on an established need for improved or additional for-hire passenger

1333 “§ 47-2895.01. Department of For-Hire Vehicles – Established.
1334 “(a) There is established the Department of For-Hire Vehicles as a subordinate agency
1335 within the executive branch of the District government with exclusive authority to regulate
1336 vehicle-for-hire and carrier-for-hire service.
1337 “(b) DFHV is charged with the continuance, further development, improvement, and
1338 overall regulation of the vehicle-for-hire and carrier-for-hire industries in the District.
1339 “(c) In addition to specific functions authorized or required by this chapter, DFHV shall
57

1340 have the authority, power, and duty to:
1341 “(1) Establish any rule relating to the regulation and supervision of for-hire
1342 passenger service not specifically delineated in this chapter, so long as the rule is consistent with
1343 this chapter and related to the furtherance and protection of the public interest;
1344 “(2) Promulgate an issuance, instruction, or guidance as it deems necessary and
1345 appropriate to aid in administration, enforcement, or compliance with any provision of this title.
1346 An issuance, instruction, or guidance may be modified or rescinded at any time with reasonable
1347 notice;
1348 “(3) Establish standards, conditions, and requirements of for-hire passenger
1349 service;
1350 “(4) Establish standards for operator and passenger safety;
1351 “(5) Establish rules relating to the enforcement of traffic regulations against
1352 vehicles covered under this chapter;
1353 “(6) Establish reasonable civil fines and penalties for violations of this chapter
1354 and any rule or order issued under this chapter, including penalties consisting of the suspension
1355 or revocation of a license or registration;
1356 “(7) Undertake the investigation of any aspect of for-hire passenger service
1357 operations and practices necessary to protect public safety;
1358 “(8) Audit records to ensure compliance with this chapter;
1359 “(9) Advise agencies and authorities with jurisdiction over public transportation,
1360 public highways, and public space within the District regarding the routing of for-hire passenger
1361 vehicles and the location of taxi stands;
1362 “(10) Advise the Mayor regarding the execution, modification, and termination of
58

1363 reciprocal agreements with other jurisdictions and authorities;
1364 “(11) Provide for the training and oversight of vehicle inspection officers;
1365 “(12) Receive, hear, respond to, and adjudicate complaints lodged against the

1367 contested matters to the Office of Administrative Hearings;
1368 “(13) Hear and decide appeals taken from license and registration denials and
1369 proposed revocations or suspensions issued pursuant to this chapter, or refer the contested matter
1370 to the Office of Administrative Hearings;
1371 “(14) When determined to be necessary to protect the public interest, hear
1372 complaints and disputes occurring within the industry, including complaints and disputes
1373 between companies, operators, or owners; and, to address industry-wide problems, issue
1374 reasonable rules for the governance of intra-industry relationships.
1375 “(d) The Department may issue orders which shall have binding effect in exercising any
1376 authority conferred by this chapter.
1377 “(e) No license requirement for operating authority shall be mandated by the Department
1378 of For-Hire Vehicles that is duplicative of the jurisdiction of the Washington Metropolitan Area
1379 Transit Commission.

1534 “§ 47-2896.03. Cease-and-desist orders.
1535 “(a) If the Department, after investigation but before a hearing, has cause to believe that a
1536 person is violating a provision of this chapter and the violation has caused, or may cause, harm to
1537 the public, the Department may issue an order requiring the alleged violator to cease and desist
1538 immediately from the violation. The order shall be served by certified mail or hand-delivered to
1539 the person.
1540 “(1) For the purposes of this subsection, engaging in or providing for-hire
1541 passenger service or dispatch service without a valid and unexpired license or endorsement shall

1543 “(b)(1) The alleged violator may, within 15 days after the service of the order, submit a
1544 written request to the Department to hold a hearing on the alleged violation.
1545 “(2) Upon receipt of a timely request, a hearing shall be held before the Office of
1546 Administrative Hearings (“OAH”) with the procedures set forth in the District of Columbia
66

1547 Administrative Procedure Act, approved October 21, 1968 (82 Stat. 1203; D.C. Official Code §
1548 2-501 et seq.), and OAH shall issue a decision within 90 days after the hearing.
1549 “(3) If a request for a hearing is not timely made under this subsection, the order
1550 of the Department shall be final.
1551 “(4) If, after a hearing, OAH determines that the alleged violator is not in
1552 violation of this chapter, the Department shall revoke the order.
1553 “(c) If a person fails to comply with a lawful order of the Department under this section,
1554 the Department may petition the Superior Court of the District of Columbia for an order
1555 compelling compliance, or take any other action authorized by this chapter.
