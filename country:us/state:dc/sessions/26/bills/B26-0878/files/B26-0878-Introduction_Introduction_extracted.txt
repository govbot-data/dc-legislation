Dear Chairman Mendelson:
Enclosed for consideration and adoption by the Council of the District of Columbia are the “Cedar
Hill Regional Medical Center Hospital Operations Agreement Second and Third Amendments
Approval Emergency Declaration Resolution of 2026” and the “Cedar Hill Regional Medical
Center Hospital Operations Agreement Second and Third Amendments Approval Emergency Act
of 2026.”
The legislation would approve two amendments to the Hospital Operations Agreement
(“Agreement”) between the Government of the District of Columbia and UHS East End Sub, LLC,
the operating entity for Cedar Hill Regional Medical Center. The amendments revise the Hospital
Fund provisions of the Agreement to establish the process for making a subsidy payment based on
the hospital’s operating-period deficit during the first year of operations and provide a support
payment for maternal and fetal care provided at the hospital to sustain labor and delivery, maternal,
fetal, and newborn care capacity.
Cedar Hill’s maternal and fetal service lines have experienced significant startup losses as the
hospital works to establish and stabilize these services. In 2025, approximately $8.6 million in
losses were reported, and in 2026 they are projected to reach $11.5 million. The losses reflect low
utilization and the substantial fixed costs associated with maintenance of the 24-hour unit. Cedar
Hill also experienced significant financial losses during its initial operating period following the
hospital’s opening in 2025; however, the Hospital Fund subsidy payment for the partial 2025
period was not previously clearly accounted for under the original agreement. The proposed
amendments authorize up to $5 million from the existing Hospital Fund, subject to the deficit-
review and fund-availability requirements of the Agreement, and a $10 million calendar year 2026
maternal and fetal care support payment.
The proposed support payments are intended to preserve critical access requirements and general
operations while Cedar Hill builds greater financial stability. I urge the Council to take prompt

and favorable action on the enclosed legislation.
If you have any questions regarding this legislation, please contact Wayne Turnage, Deputy Mayor
for Health and Human Services.
Sincerely,
Muriel Bowser

between the District and Cedar Hill, which was approved by Council in November 2020.4 The
Agreement requires the District to provide an operating reserve of $25 million in the Hospital Fund
to be paid to Cedar Hill for operating deficits of up to $5 million annually, beginning the first full year
after Cedar Hill started operations.5 The Hospital Fund6 can only be used to reduce documented
Cedar Hill operating deficits over a ten-year period. Any remaining funding in the Hospital Fund
expires after ten years. Cedar Hill must provide a “deficit notification” to the District every year by
March 1, indicating whether Cedar Hill (including its parking facility) had an operating deficit in the
previous fiscal year requiring the use of the reserve funding. The notification must include relevant
financial statements and other supporting documentation.
The Agreement was first amended in April 20257, but the first amendment’s changes did not affect
funding plans.
The bill approves the second and third amendments (“Amendments”) to the Agreement, executed on
September 29, 2026 and October 20, 2026 respectively. The Amendments are conditioned on Council
approval of the Amendments by December 31, 2026.
First, the Amendments allow the Agreement’s initial $5 million operating deficit payment from the
Hospital Fund to be paid earlier, to reduce Cedar Hill’s operating deficit in the first partial year of
operations (April 2025 to December 2025). Cedar Hill must provide the deficit notice and supporting
documentation for this initial operating period by November 15, 2026. The Amendments also change
the required timing of future deficit notifications from March 1 to April 1 to better align with Cedar
Hill’s January to December fiscal year and the closing of its books.
Second, the Amendments authorize up to $10 million in additional funding in fiscal year 2026 to
reduce the operating deficit of Cedar Hill’s maternal and fetal care service lines. The payment is
subject to compliance requirements and cannot exceed the net operating loss of the maternal and
fetal care services. The compliance requirements include service and quality standards and
continued operation of labor and delivery services, including neonatal intensive care and continuous
obstetric physician coverage, through December 31, 2026.
Lastly, the Amendments clarify that the District may transfer and use any remaining funds in the
Hospital Fund at the end of the ten-year term for any purpose.
