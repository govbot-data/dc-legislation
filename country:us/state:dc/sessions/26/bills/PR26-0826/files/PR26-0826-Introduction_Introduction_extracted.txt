15 To declare the existence of an emergency with respect to the need to clarify that a transition
16 committee may be organized on behalf of the Mayor-elect and to adopt the definition of
17 Mayor-elect provided in the Mayoral Transition Amendment Act of 2026 to the Board of
18 Ethics and Government Accountability Establishment and Comprehensive Ethics Reform
19 Amendment Act of 2011 and to the Official Correspondence Regulations.
20
21 RESOLVED, BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this
22 resolution may be cited as the “Mayoral Transition Emergency Declaration Resolution of 2026”.
23 Sec. 2. (a) The Board of Ethics and Government Accountability Establishment and
24 Comprehensive Ethics Reform Amendment Act of 2011 uses the term “Mayor-elect” but does
25 not define it. Further, the definition of “transition committee” allows a committee to be
26 organized on behalf of the Mayor but does not expressly mention the Mayor-elect.
27 (b) The Official Correspondence Regulations also use, but lack a definition of, the term
28 “Mayor-elect”.
29 (c) These omissions create uncertainty about when a person qualifies as Mayor-elect, if a
30 transition committee can be organized on behalf of the Mayor-elect, and whether and when the
31 Mayor-elect may mail materials as official mail.
32 (d) In the Mayoral Transition Act of 2026, the Council defined “Mayor-elect” as a person
33 certified by the Board of Elections as the successful candidate of the 2026 general election or,
1

34 before certification, the person announced and published by the Board of Elections as the
35 unofficial winner of the 2026 general election with a margin of victory of at least 2% of the votes
36 cast. That definition, however, was not incorporated into the Board of Ethics and Government
37 Accountability Establishment and Comprehensive Ethics Reform Amendment Act of 2011 or the
38 Official Correspondence Regulations.
39 (e) This legislation clarifies existing law by incorporating that definition into the ethics
40 and campaign finance provisions, expressly includes “Mayor-elect” in the definition of
41 “transition committee,” and applies the same definition to the provision authorizing the Mayor-
42 elect to use official mail.
43 (f) With the November 2026 general election approaching, these ambiguities must be
44 resolved before the transition begins. As the October 20th Additional Legislative Meeting is the
45 last chance the Council will have to make these statutory clarifications, emergency legislation is
46 necessary to provide clear and consistent rules for the Mayor-elect’s transition activities during
47 the limited period between the election, certification, and the beginning of the next mayoral term.
48 Sec. 3. The Council of the District of Columbia determines that the circumstances
49 enumerated in section 2 constitute emergency circumstances making it necessary that the
50 Mayoral Transition Emergency Amendment Act of 2026 be adopted after a single reading.
51 Sec. 4. This resolution shall take effect immediately.
