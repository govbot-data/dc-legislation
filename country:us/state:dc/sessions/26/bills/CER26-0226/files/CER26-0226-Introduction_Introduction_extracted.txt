12 To declare the month of October 2026 as “Domestic Violence Awareness Month” in the District
13 of Columbia.
14
15 WHEREAS, protecting and improving the dignity and safety of all residents is the
16 foundation of a vibrant and healthy community;
17 WHEREAS, domestic violence is a pattern of abusive behavior used to exert power and
18 control over an intimate partner that jeopardizes the security and well-being of District residents,
19 and such destructive acts will not be tolerated;
20 WHEREAS, all forms of domestic violence—including physical, psychological,
21 emotional, and economic abuse—can have devastating consequences for survivors and their
22 families;
23 WHEREAS, 41 percent of women and 21 percent of men in the District have experienced
24 intimate partner physical violence, sexual violence, or stalking in their lifetimes;
25 WHEREAS, approximately 840 million women across the world have experienced
26 physical or sexual violence by an intimate partner or sexual violence from a non-partner;
27 WHEREAS, nearly 99 percent of survivors experienced economic abuse from their partner,
28 with many also facing long-term financial consequences as a result of intimate partner violence;
29 WHEREAS, in a one-day snapshot in 2025, 790 survivors received help from domestic
30 violence service providers in Washington, D.C., with 339 of those survivors receiving emergency
31 shelter and housing;
32 WHEREAS, during that same one-day snapshot, 103 additional survivors called domestic
33 violence hotlines;
2

34 WHEREAS, in that same 24-hour period, providers were nevertheless unable to meet
35 approximately 12 survivors’ requests for support due to insufficient resources, funding, and/or
36 staffing;
37 WHEREAS, of District residents experiencing homelessness in 2026, 44 percent of adults
38 in families and 22 percent of single adults reported some history of domestic violence
39 victimization;
40 WHEREAS, 40 percent of homeless youth in the District indicate that they have
41 experienced domestic violence;
42 WHEREAS, less than 1 percent of permanent supportive housing units in the District are
43 dedicated to survivors of domestic violence;
44 WHEREAS, according to the 2017 D.C. Women’s Needs Assessment Report, Black
45 women, who comprise only 52 percent of the adult women’s population, accounted for 75 percent
46 of the District’s unaccompanied homeless women’s population;
47 WHEREAS, domestic violence service providers continue to report that their survivor
48 services are in very high demand;
49 WHEREAS, economic stability and access to financial resources can be a critical factor in
50 a survivor’s ability to leave an abuser or dangerous environment;
51 WHEREAS, the District must commit to addressing the racial, social, and economic
52 inequities that are uniquely harmful to communities of color and that hinder many survivors from
53 thriving after leaving an abusive relationship;
54 WHEREAS, there is a continuing need for survivors to have access to culturally specific
55 services that affirm their heritage, customs, sexual orientation, gender identity, and other lived
56 experiences;
3

57 WHEREAS, language access barriers make access and engagement with services more
58 difficult for Deaf and limited- and non-English proficient survivors in the District and
59 elsewhere;
60 WHEREAS, service providers should receive adequate funding and support from local,
61 regional, and federal governments to address the growing demand and greater need for housing,
62 counseling, legal, and culturally specific services;
63 WHEREAS, the goal of Domestic Violence Awareness Month is to improve awareness of
64 domestic violence and outreach to residents and community members, and foster cooperation
65 between public and private actors to end domestic violence in the District;
66 WHEREAS, the District can do much more to support survivors and service providers, and
67 the Council will continue to partner with District agencies, community-based organizations, and
68 residents to keep survivors safe.
69 RESOLVED, BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this
70 resolution may be cited as the “Domestic Violence Awareness Month Recognition Resolution of
71 2026”.
72 Sec. 2. The Council declares the month of October 2026 as “Domestic Violence Awareness
73 Month” in the District of Columbia.
74 Sec. 3. This resolution shall take effect immediately upon the first date of publication in
75 the District of Columbia Register.
