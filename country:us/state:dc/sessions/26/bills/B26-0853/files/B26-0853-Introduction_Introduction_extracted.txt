Statement of Introduction on the
Transparency in Downcoding Amendment Act of 2026
October 5, 2026
Today I, along with Chairman Phil Mendelson, Councilmembers Charles Allen, Anita
Bonds, Matthew Frumin, Janeese Lewis George, Brianne K. Nadeau, Zachary Parker, Brooke
Pinto, and Trayon White, Sr., am introducing the Transparency in Downcoding Amendment Act
of 2026. This legislation protects District of Columbia clinicians and patients from the growing
practice of health insurers automatically downcoding medical claims and reducing payment for
care that has been provided. Downcoding occurs when an insurer changes a service code submitted
by a clinician to a lower-level code, resulting in lower reimbursement. Increasingly, insurers are
using automated systems, algorithms, and artificial intelligence to identify and downcode claims,
sometimes without a clinical professional reviewing the clinical record or the information
submitted by the treating clinician. This practice can undermine clinical judgment, impose
significant administrative burdens on medical practices, and unfairly penalize clinicians who care
for patients with complex or chronic conditions.
This legislation would prohibit insurers from automatically downcoding claims using
artificial intelligence or other automated tools when the decision is based solely on a reported
diagnosis code. It would also establish basic transparency and due-process protections: when an
insurer downcodes a claim, the clinician must be clearly notified that the claim was downcoded,
provided with the reason for the change, and given a clear and accessible process to appeal the
decision. The bill would further prohibit insurers from using downcoding practices to target
clinicians who routinely care for patients with complex or chronic conditions.
This problem extends beyond the District; automatic downcoding has become a growing
national concern. The American Medical Association has reported widespread use of payer
downcoding programs, sharing examples of how insurers are increasingly using automated
downcoding programs to reduce physician payments without adequate notice or review of the
patient's medical record.1 Other states are acting: Illinois,2 Indiana,3 and Virginia,4 have enacted
legislation prohibiting insurers from using an automated process, including artificial intelligence,
as the sole basis for downcoding without clinical review, while also requiring notice, an appeals
process, and protections against targeting clinicians who treat complex patients. In addition to

The District of Columbia should not wait for these practices to become more entrenched.
Clinicians should be paid accurately for the care they provide, and insurers should not be permitted
to substitute a diagnosis code reviewed by an algorithm in lieu of clinical judgment and
documentation of the treating professional without a justification. This legislation will bring
greater transparency, accountability, and fairness to health care reimbursement in the District while
ensuring that patients with complex and chronic conditions are not indirectly disadvantaged by
insurance payment practices. I encourage my colleagues to support this legislation.

37 To amend the Prompt Pay Act of 2002 to prohibit health insurers from automatically
38 downcoding a claim using artificial intelligence and based solely on the reported
39 diagnosis code; to require health insurers who downcode claims to notify the clinician to
40 clearly indicate the claim had been downcoded; to provide clinicians with a clear and
41 accessible process for appealing downcoded claims; to not target clinicians who routinely
42 treat patients with complex or chronic conditions; and to make violations of this act
43 enforceable by the Department.
44
45 BE IT ENACTED BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this
46 Act may be cited as the “Transparency in Downcoding Amendment Act of 2026”.

48 Sec. 2. The Prompt Pay Act of 2002, effective July 23, 2002 (D.C. Law 14-176; D.C.
49 Official Code § 31-3131 et seq.), is amended as follows:
50 (a) Section 2 (D.C. Official Code § 31-3131) is amended as follows:
51 (1) The existing paragraph (1) is redesignated as paragraph (1A).
52 (2) A new paragraph (1) is added to read as follows:
53 “(1) “CARC” means the Claim Adjustment Reason Codes, which provide the
54 reason for a financial adjustment specific to a particular claim or service referenced in the
55 Accredited Standards Committee X12 835 standard transaction adopted by the Department of
56 Health and Human Services under 45 CFR § 162.1602.”.
57 (3) A new paragraph (3A) is added to read as follows:
58 “(3A) “Downcoding” means the unilateral alteration by a health insurer of the
59 level of evaluation and management service code or other service code submitted on a claim,
60 resulting in a lower payment to the provider.”.
61 (4) Paragraph (5) is amended by striking the period and inserting the phrase “,
62 including a third-party administrator or other payer responsible for adjudicating claims on behalf
63 of a health insurer.”.
64 (5) A new paragraph (9) is added to read as follows:
65 “(9) “RARC” means the Remittance Advice Remark Codes, which provide
66 supplemental information about a financial adjustment indicated by a CARC or information
67 about remittance processing.”.
68 (b) A new section 5a is added to read as follows:
69 “Sec. 5a. Prohibition on downcoding.
70 “(a) A health insurer shall not:

71 “(1) Use an automated process, system, or tool to downcode a claim, including the
72 use of artificial intelligence;
73 “(2) Downcode a claim based solely on the reported diagnosis code; or
74 “(3) Use downcoding practices in a targeted or discriminatory manner against a
75 physician who routinely treat patients with complex or chronic conditions.”.
76 “(b) All downcoding decisions shall be made by a clinician licensed in the District and of
77 the same specialty as the treating provider who shall perform a documented review of the clinical
78 information supporting the billed service.
79 “(c) When a claim is downcoded, the health insurer shall notify the physician using the
80 appropriate CARC and RARC to clearly indicate that the claim has been downcoded and
81 provide:
82 “(1) The specific reason for the downcoding, including by reference to the clinical
83 criteria used to justify the downcoding;
84 “(2) The original and revised service codes and payment amounts;
85 “(3) The National Provider Identifier of the physician responsible for the
86 downcoding decision, as well as the physician’s credentials, board certifications, and areas of
87 specialty expertise and training; and
88 “(4) A notice of the right to appeal as provided in subsection (d) of this section.
89 “(d)(1) A health insurer shall provide providers with a clear and accessible process for
90 appealing downcoded claims, including a written or electronic notice detailing how to initiate an
91 appeal, contact information for the individual managing the appeal, reasonable timelines for
92 submission of an appeal that are no less than 180 days, and timelines for the adjudication of the
93 appeal.

94 “(2) A provider shall have the right to appeal in batches of similar claims
95 involving substantially similar downcoding issues, without restriction.
96 “(e) Any pattern or practice of discriminatory downcoding identified by the
97 Commissioner or other regulatory authority shall be subject to enforcement actions, including
98 fines, restitution, or suspension of health insurer licensure in the District.
99 “(f) Violations of this section shall be enforceable by the Commissioner and may include:
100 “(1) Monetary penalties of up to $50,000 per violation; and
101 “(2) Orders to reprocess improperly downcoded claims with interest.”.
