ENROLLED ORIGINAL
A RESOLUTION
26-536
IN THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA
October 20, 2026
To declare the existence of an emergency with respect to the need to amend the Board of Ethics
and Government Accountability Establishment and Comprehensive Ethics Reform
Amendment Act of 2011 to clarify that a transition committee may be organized on
behalf of the Mayor-elect and to adopt the definition of “Mayor-elect” provided in the
Mayoral Transition Act of 2026; and to amend the Official Correspondence Regulations
to adopt the same definition of “Mayor-elect”.
RESOLVED, BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this
resolution may be cited as the “Mayoral Transition Emergency Declaration Resolution of 2026”.
Sec. 2. (a) The Board of Ethics and Government Accountability Establishment and
Comprehensive Ethics Reform Amendment Act of 2011, effective April 27, 2012 (D.C. Law 19-
124; D.C. Official Code § 1-1161.01 et seq.), uses the term “Mayor-elect” but does not define it.
Further, the definition of “transition committee” allows a committee to be organized on behalf of
the Mayor but does not expressly mention the Mayor-elect.
(b) The Official Correspondence Regulations, effective April 7, 1977 (D.C. Law 1-118;
D.C. Official Code § 2-701 et seq.), also use, but lack a definition of, the term “Mayor-elect”.
(c) These omissions create uncertainty about when a person qualifies as Mayor-elect, if a
transition committee can be organized on behalf of the Mayor-elect, and whether and when the
Mayor-elect may mail materials as official mail.
(d) In the Mayoral Transition Act of 2026, effective October 2, 2026 (D.C. Law 26-189;
73 DCR 11735), the Council defined “Mayor-elect” as a person certified by the Board of
Elections as the successful candidate of the 2026 general election or, before certification, the
person announced and published by the Board of Elections as the unofficial winner of the 2026
general election with a margin of victory of at least 2% of the votes cast. That definition,
however, was not incorporated into the Board of Ethics and Government Accountability
Establishment and Comprehensive Ethics Reform Amendment Act of 2011, effective April 27,
2012 (D.C. Law 19-124; D.C. Official Code § 1-1161.01 et seq.), or the Official Correspondence
Regulations, effective April 7, 1977 (D.C. Law 1-118; D.C. Official Code § 2-701 et seq.).
(e) The accompanying emergency legislation clarifies existing law by incorporating that
definition into the ethics and campaign finance provisions, expressly includes “Mayor-elect” in

(f) With the November 2026 general election approaching, these ambiguities must be
resolved before the transition begins. As the October 20 Additional Legislative Meeting is the
last chance the Council will have to make these statutory clarifications, emergency legislation is
necessary to provide clear and consistent rules for the Mayor-elect’s transition activities during
the limited period between the election, certification, and the beginning of the next mayoral term.
Sec. 3. The Council of the District of Columbia determines that the circumstances
enumerated in section 2 constitute emergency circumstances making it necessary that the
Mayoral Transition Emergency Amendment Act of 2026 be adopted after a single reading.
Sec. 4. This resolution shall take effect immediately.
