Dear Chairman Mendelson:
Enclosed for consideration and enactment by the Council of the District of Columbia is an
emergency bill, the “Contract No. CW130810 with Capitol Paving of D.C., Inc. and
Modification Nos. M01 and M02, Approval and Payment Authorization Emergency Act of
2026,” and the accompanying emergency declaration resolution.
The legislation will approve emergency Contract No. CW130810 with Capitol Paving of D.C.,
Inc. and Modification No. M01 and M02, to provide equipment and qualified operators for
snow plowing and removal for the period from November 7, 2026 through November 6, 2027.
The legislation will approve payment for the goods and services received and to be received
under the contract in the not-to-exceed amount of $5,000,000.
My administration is available to discuss any questions you may have regarding the proposed
contract modification. In order to facilitate a response to any questions you may have, please have
your staff contact Marc Scott, Chief Operating Officer, Office of Contracting and Procurement, at
(202) 724-8759.
I urge the Council to take prompt and favorable action on the enclosed legislation.
Sincerely,
Muriel Bowser

(L) The reason why the proposed change was sent to Council for approval after it is intended to
take effect:
There was not sufficient time to seek Council approval first. The Mayor issued a Declaration of
Public Emergency on January 23, 2026, under Mayor’s Order 2026‑005, within which a Snow
Emergency beginning January 24, 2026, at noon was declared. This emergency declaration
occurred in the middle of the standard internal routing process for submission to the Council. The
emergency conditions required immediate action to ensure uninterrupted Department of Public
Works operations, and there was a significant risk that the letter contract’s not‑to‑exceed limit of
$1,000,000 would be insufficient to meet the operational demands of the Snow Emergency.

SECTION C: SPECIFICATIONS/WORK STATEMENT
C.1 SCOPE:
The District of Columbia Government, Department of Public Works requires the services of a
Contractor to perform snow and iceremoval to ensure that the roadways are passable as specified
herein. The source of the roadway coverage is the National Highway System (NHS) within the
District of Columbia as shown in Attachment J.10.
The Contractor shall provide all supervision, personnel, equipment, materials, tools, supplies and
all incidentals required to remove ice and snowfromDistrictstreetsdesignated underthe National
Highway System. The District will provide de-icing sodium chloride rock salt and brine for pre-
treating.

C.3 DEFINITIONS
These terms when used in this IFB havethefollowing meanings:
C.3.1 Snow Event (Event) – is a Snow, Ice or Anti-icing Event declared by the District in which the
Contractor is required to perform all requirements of the contract, more specifically provide all
required equipment and equipment operators as ordered by the Contract Administrator (CA).
C.4 BACKGROUND
The District has a recurring need to ensure the safety of the traveling public through the
provision of snow and ice control services on the National Highway System (NHS). These
services have been satisfied over the last seven years, through the employment of a Contractor
that provided the required equipment, equipment operators and the resources required to keep
all such equipment operational during the declared “Event”.
It is estimated that during the snow season the District will experience fifteen (15) snow events
that last for approximately forty-eight (48) hours each. The total mileage for NHS is approximately
seventy-six (76) miles consisting of 334 lane miles.

the District will compensate the Contractor for a minimum of four (4) hours for the
CLINS that DPW ordered for that event.
A DDOT/DPW supervisor per zone will be assigned to provide instruction to the
Contractor’s personnel and equipment operation during each event. If the duration of
the snow event requires it, the Contractor shall be responsible for providing relief
drivers in order to sustain 24 hour around the clock operation without down time for
removal of snow on District NHS routes.

without a replacement, liquidated damages will be applied. See Section H.14

C.5.2.3 DRY RUN
One “dry-run” (100%readinessmobilizationof Contractor’s equipment and personnel)
will be conducted each snow season. On a predetermined date, usually in October or
November, the District will conduct a dry-run test of the entire snow program. The dry-
run is required to identify any weaknesses in the overall plan. It isan exercisethatwill
demonstrate that each plow/spreader truck and chemical spray tanker truck is
operational and each driver demonstrates knowledge of his assignment. Each driver
will be given the opportunity to drive his assigned route entirely and to identify any
potential roadway obstacles prior to an Event. The event will last at least 4 hours.
Equipment operators shall be required to have all required communication devices
during the dry-run and be able to communicate with DPW supervisors. All
requirements outlined in the contract that are necessary during an actual storm event
shall be in place and operational during the dry run.
The equipment required is what the District would call a full deployment. The
equipment for the dry-run typically includes, 33 large plow/spreaders with operator, 5
pick-ups with spreader and plow and operator, and 5 spray tankers with operator with
plows. Front loaders, haul trucks, and roll-off haul trucks with 40 CY boxes with
operator shall not be required at the dry run.
The District reserves the right toadjustthe minimum amount of equipment and type of
equipment at anytime.

require the use of loaders.

Haul trucks shall be 10-wheel dump, 6x4, minimum 64,000 lbs. with dump bed minimum
capacity of 17 cubic yards.
