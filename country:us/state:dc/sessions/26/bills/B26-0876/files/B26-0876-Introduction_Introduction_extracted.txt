Statement of Introduction
Thurgood Marshall Center Trust Inc., Tax Exemption Amendment Act
October 20, 2026
Today I am introducing the “Thurgood Marshall Center Trust, Inc., Tax Exemption Amendment
Act of 2026”. This legislation provides a tax exemption for the Thurgood Marshall Center for
Service and Heritage located at 1816 12th Street, N.W. in Ward 1 to ensure its continued place in
the culture and history of the District of Columbia.
Anthony Bowen, an enslaved person who bought his freedom, founded the first full-service
YMCA for African Americans in the United States in 1853 in a building on 11th Street, N.W.
By 1912, the organization moved into its permanent home at 1816 12th Street, N.W., and was
known as the Twelfth Street YMCA. Today, owned and operated by the Thurgood Marshall
Center Trust, Inc. (“Trust”), the building now known as the Thurgood Marshall Center for
Service and Heritage (“TMSC”) is a significant piece of the District’s history. It is one of very
few landmarks of its stature that remains owned and operated by an African American-led
nonprofit organization.
For generations of Black residents barred from the District’s other institutions, the building
served as gymnasium, dormitory, meeting hall, and refuge; it appeared in the Negro Motorist
Green Book as safe lodging for Black travelers passing through a segregated capital. Its place in
our national history is secure. Some of the most notable African American civil rights leaders
used the building to strategize the greatest achievements in American history. Thurgood
Marshall and Charles Hamilton Houston worked in this building as they built the legal plan that
led to Brown v. Board of Education. Langston Hughes, Dr. Charles Drew, Joe Louis, and John
Thompson all passed through its doors. The building was listed in the National Register of
Historic Places on October 3, 1983, and designated a National Historic Landmark on October 12,
1994. The building was named to the White House Millennium Council’s “Save America’s
Treasures” list. It is also a contributing property in the Greater U Street Historic District and a
stop on the African American Heritage Trail.
The building still stands today as the result of decades of neighborhood organizing. After the
YMCA closed the facility in 1982, a restoration effort led by Delegate Walter Fauntroy and
Shaw residents kept it from being lost to history. The Trust was incorporated in 1990, launched a
capital campaign in 1995, and reopened the restored landmark to the public in February 2000
carrying Justice Marshall’s name, with the blessing of his wife Cecilia Marshall.
Now, years later, this building is active again. It provides space to many nonprofit organizations
serving District residents — literacy programs, youth mentoring, family support, and civic
groups — alongside heritage programming, a gymnasium, and meeting and event space used by
Shaw neighbors, ANC commissions, and community organizations.

But preserving 35,000 square feet of a century-old landmark of this size is expensive. And
although the Trust leases 28,500 square feet of the space to nonprofit tenants at below market
rents, the real property tax assessments and liability directly work against both the affordability
the community organizations rely on, as well as the larger cultural preservation effort. TMSC sits
in the Shaw neighborhood, which has changed profoundly in recent decades. Property taxes
steadily increase as assessments continue to rise. Assessments rise with neighborhood values,
even though the Trust’s income does not. Institutions founded, built, and owned by Black
Washingtonians have been steadily lost to that cycle of change.
For these reasons, this legislation exempts the building from real property taxation and
assessments for charitable purposes and activities. Ensuring that the Thurgood Marshall Center
Trust can hold and maintain this landmark without the annual burden of a tax bill on its building,
we can ensure that this vital institution continues to serve and contribute to the Shaw
neighborhood and to the District of Columbia.

A BILL
_________________________
IN THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA
_________________________
1 To amend Title 47 of the District of Columbia Code to provide for a property tax exemption for
2 the real property situated on Lot 0055, Square 0275 at 1816 12th Street, N.W., known as
3 the Twelfth Street YMCA Building and operated as the Thurgood Marshall Center for
4 Service and Heritage.
5
6 BE IT ENACTED BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this
7 act may be cited as the “Thurgood Marshall Center Trust, Inc. Real Property Tax Exemption
8 Amendment Act of 2026”.
9 Sec. 2. Chapter 10 of Title 47 of the District of Columbia Code is amended as follows:
10 (a) The table of contents is amended by adding a new section designation to read as
11 follows:
12 “§ 47-1099.18. Thurgood Marshall Center Trust, Inc.; Lot 0055, Square 0275.
13 (b) A new section 47-1099.18 is added to read as follows:
14 “(a) The real property, and improvements on the property, located at 1816 12th Street,
15 N.W., known as the Twelfth Street YMCA Building and operated as the Thurgood Marshall
16 Center for Service and Heritage, known for tax and assessment purposes as Lot 0055, Square
17 0275 (“Property”), shall be exempt from the tax imposed by Chapter 8 of this title, for the period

18 of January 1, 2021 through December 31, 2026; provided, that the property continues to be
19 owned by the Thurgood Marshall Center Trust, Inc., for the purposes of charitable activities.
20 “(b) Any transfer, assignment, or other disposition of all or any portion of the real
21 property described in subsection (a) of this section, including an assignment of leasehold interest
22 in the real property or a sublease of the real property shall be exempt from the tax imposed under
23 §§ 47-801-47-895.35.
24 “(b) Real property taxes, interest, penalties, fees, and other related charges assessed, as of
25 the effective date of this act, shall be forgiven, and any payments made for such period shall be
26 refunded.”.
27 Sec. 3. Applicability.
28 This act shall apply as of October 1, 2026.
29 Sec. 4. Fiscal impact statement.
30 The Council adopts the fiscal impact statement in the committee report as the fiscal
31 impact statement required by section 4a of the General Legislative Procedures Act of 1975,
32 approved October 16, 2006 (120 Stat. 2038; D.C. Official Code § 1-301.47a).
33 Sec. 4. Effective date.
34 This act shall take effect after approval by the Mayor (or in the event of veto by the
35 Mayor, action by the Council to override the veto), a 30-day period of congressional review as
36 provided in section 602(c)(1) of the District of Columbia Home Rule Act, approved December
37 24, 1973 (87 Stat. 813; D.C. Official Code § 1-206.02(c)(1)), and publication in the District of
38 Columbia Register.
