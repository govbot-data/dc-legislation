Please find attached the Health-Care Decisions Amendment Act of 2026 for enactment by the Council of
the District of Columbia.
If enacted, the proposed legislation would amend Chapter 22 of Title 21 of the District of Columbia Official
Code to expand the range of health professionals authorized to assist patients in completing, executing, and
complying with a Medical Orders for Scope of Treatment (MOST) Form, ensuring that patients can more
easily document their treatment preferences and that providers have clearer, more consistent guidance when
honoring those decisions.
Specifically, the legislation would expand the list of professionals qualified to assist with MOST Forms by
including advanced practice nurses, naturopathic physicians, and physician assistants acting under
delegation agreements; clarify which qualified health professionals may review and execute MOST Forms
with patients or their authorized representatives; revise the composition of the MOST Advisory Committee
to include members of the District’s healthcare system and update member term lengths; and streamline the
MOST Form process by clarifying which qualified health professionals may review and execute the form
with a patient or authorized representative and requiring that completed MOST Forms, where applicable
and available, be uploaded into the patient’s electronic medical record and treated as part of the permanent
medical record unless revoked.
These updates strengthen the District’s system for documenting and honoring patient treatment preferences.
They also improve clarity for both providers and patients during medical decision-making and ensure that
MOST Forms are accessible across care settings through modernized electronic recordkeeping.
I urge the Council to take prompt and favorable action on the Health-Care Decisions Amendment Act of
2026.

Conclusion
Funds are sufficient in the fiscal year 2026 budget and proposed fiscal year 2027 through fiscal year
2030 budget and financial plan to implement the bill.
Background
The Medical Orders for Scope of Treatment (MOST) program, administered by the Department of
Health (DC Health), allows terminally ill patients to make decisions about their end-of-life care
options in consultation with a District-licensed physician or advanced practice registered nurse.
Patients complete a paper or electronic MOST Form that specifies their preferences for
cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), medical interventions/treatment options, antibiotics, and
medically-assisted nutrition.
The bill makes several changes1 to how the MOST Form is completed and executed, including:
• Allowing naturopathic physicians and physician assistants acting under a valid delegation
agreement to assist in the process for completing, executing, and complying with a MOST
Form;

• Clarifying who can complete a MOST Form by eliminating the requirement that a person have

• Changes the composition and duties of the MOST Advisory Committee; and
• Requires that MOST Forms be included in a patient’s permanent electronic medical record.
Financial Plan Impact
Funds are sufficient in the fiscal year 2026 budget and proposed fiscal year 2027 through fiscal year
2030 budget and financial plan to implement the bill. Expanding who can complete and execute a
MOST Form, changing the composition and duties of the MOST Advisory Committee, and requiring
that MOST Forms be included in a patient’s electronic medical record do not have a cost. DC Health
can implement the bill with existing resources.
