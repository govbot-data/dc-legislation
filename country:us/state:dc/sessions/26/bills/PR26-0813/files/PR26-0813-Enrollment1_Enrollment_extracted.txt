ENROLLED ORIGINAL
A RESOLUTION
26-523
IN THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA
October 6, 2026
To declare the existence of an emergency with respect to the need to amend the Block Party Act
of 2012 to enable residents hosting a musical performance on their property as part of a
porchfest to apply for block party permits.
RESOLVED, BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this
resolution may be cited as the “Porchfest Permitting Emergency Declaration Resolution of
2026”.
Sec. 2. (a) The Porchfest Permitting Emergency Amendment Act of 2025, effective
December 30, 2025 (D.C. Act 26-236; 73 DCR 66), was passed by the Council on December 2,
2025 to ensure that porchfest performances hosted by individual residents in Spring 2025 could
apply for block party permits. The accompanying temporary legislation, the Porchfest Permitting
Temporary Amendment Act of 2025, effective March 6, 2026 (D.C. Law 26-99; 73 DCR 448),
was passed by the Council on December 16, 2025, is set to expire on October 17, 2026.
(b) Permanent legislation, the Porchfest Permitting Amendment Act of 2025, as
introduced on October 7, 2025 (Bill 26-424), is pending before the Council. A hearing was held
on September 24, 2026. Emergency legislation is therefore necessary to provide continuity while
the permanent legislation proceeds through the Council’s legislative process.
(c) This fall, two porchfest festivals are scheduled to take place, one after the date of the
current temporary legislation’s expiration: Capitol Hill’s inaugural Porchfest on October 10,
followed by Adams Morgan’s Fall Porchfest on November 7. Organizers across the District are
also in the early stages of planning their spring porchfests. Ensuring continuity and certainty in
the permitting process is necessary so that both the upcoming fall festivals and those planned for
the spring can move forward without disruption.
(d) The Block Party Act of 2012, effective October 23, 2012 (D.C. Law 19-190, D.C.
Official Code § 9-631 et seq.), was enacted to create a simple, accessible process for residents to
obtain permission to host neighborhood block parties.
(e) Prior to the passage of the emergency and temporary legislation, residents who
applied for block party permits to host porchfest musical performances were denied because the
events were considered part of a larger porchfest festival. They were told they must instead apply
for a special event permit, which is used for major, professionally organized events and is
significantly more costly and complex.

(f) Emergency legislation is necessary to ensure continuity for porchfest organizers for
upcoming fall and spring porchfests while permanent legislation makes its way through the

Sec. 3. The Council of the District of Columbia determines that the circumstances
enumerated in section 2 constitute emergency circumstances making it necessary that the
Porchfest Permitting Emergency Amendment Act of 2026 be adopted after a single reading.
Sec. 4. This resolution shall take effect immediately.
