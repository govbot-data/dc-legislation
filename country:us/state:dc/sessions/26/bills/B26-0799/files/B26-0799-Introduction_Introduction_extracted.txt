Enclosed for consideration and adoption by the Council of the District of Columbia is the
HealthCare Facilities Regulation Amendment Act of 2026.
If enacted, the proposed legislation would amend several statutes governing the oversight,
licensure, and regulation of health care facilities and entities in the District to strengthen patient
safety, modernize regulatory authority, and improve transparency and accountability across the
health care system. Specifically, the bill would amend the Health-Care and Community
Residence Facility and Home Care Licensure Act of 1983, the Medical Malpractice Amendment
Act of 2006, the Health Services Planning Program Re-establishment Act of 1996, and related
provisions of District law to accomplish the following:
Regulatory Modernization and Alignment with Federal Standards: The legislation clarifies
and expands the District’s authority to incorporate applicable federal standards, including
Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services. These updates will promote regulatory consistency,
reduce provider confusion, and ensure health care facilities operate in accordance with nationally
recognized safety and quality standards.
Strengthened Enforcement and Patient Safety Protections: The bill enhances the Department
of Health’s enforcement tools by authorizing escalated and proportional civil penalties when
facility conduct places patients, residents, or clients in immediate jeopardy, even where actual
harm has not yet occurred. These changes better align the District with national enforcement
practices and allow for timely intervention to prevent serious injury or loss of life.
Health Care Facility Oversight: The legislation strengthens oversight of assisted living
residences by establishing clearer licensure requirements for facilities providing higher-acuity
care.
Ownership Transparency and Financial Accountability: To address increasing consolidation
and financialization in the health care sector, the bill enhances disclosure requirements related to

ownership and control, including private equity and other complex ownership structures. These
provisions improve transparency and support more informed regulatory review of transactions
that may impact access, quality, or stability of care.
Licensure Flexibility and Administrative Efficiency: The bill modernizes licensure and
renewal processes by updating Clean Hands requirements, authorizing provisional licensure in
limited circumstances, and permitting fee adjustments tied to inflation through public notice.
These changes promote administrative efficiency while maintaining appropriate safeguards for
patient safety.
Modernized Adverse Event Reporting: The legislation updates adverse event reporting
requirements to include cybersecurity incidents, clarifies reporting definitions, and strengthens
participation in patient safety organizations, supporting improved detection and prevention of
systemic safety issues.
Taken together, these reforms strengthen the District’s health care oversight framework, support
provider readiness, enhance patient protections, and promote a more coordinated and equitable
health care system for District residents.
I urge the Council to take prompt and favorable action on the enclosed legislation.

Conclusion
Funds are not sufficient in the proposed revised fiscal year 2026 budget and proposed fiscal year
2027 through fiscal year 2030 budget and financial plan to implement the bill. The Department of
Health (DC Health) requires $155,000 in fiscal year 2027 and $642,000 over the financial plan to
implement the bill.
Background
The bill makes several changes to the regulation and oversight of healthcare providers and facilities
in the District of Columbia. Specifically, the bill:
• Requires1 healthcare providers to report adverse events2 and cybersecurity incidents to DC
Health within 48 hours of their occurrence or discovery;
1 By amending Section 202 of the Medical Malpractice Amendment Act of 2006, effective March 14, 2007 (D.C.
Law 16-263; D.C. Official Code § 7-161 et seq.).
2 An adverse event is defined in the bill as a preventable event, occurrence, or situation occurring in or under
the purview of a healthcare provider that results in death or contributes to harm that includes physical,
emotional, or psychological harm that requires major intervention, or impairs the individual’s ability to
perform activities of daily living. The term shall include any other events established by the Mayor through
rulemaking.

DC Health must hire one Nurse Specialist to conduct inspections that result from an increase in
adverse event reporting. DC Health requires additional investigative capacity to review adverse
event reports and assist with investigations. DC Health already maintains a centralized system to
catalog adverse event reports, so no additional resources are needed to procure one. The Nurse
Specialist salary and fringe benefit costs are $155,000 in fiscal year 2027 and $642,000 over the
financial plan. DC Health can implement the provisions in the bill that do not pertain to adverse event
reporting with existing resources.
