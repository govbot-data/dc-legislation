7 A CEREMONIAL RESOLUTION
8
9 ______________
10
11 IN THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA
12
13 __________________
14
15 To proclaim the Month of October, 2026 as "DC End Addiction Stigma Month" to raise
16 awareness of the ways addiction stigma can obstruct access to care and to ensure that individuals
17 with substance use disorder can reach respectful treatment and recovery support.
18
19 WHEREAS, DC Ends Addiction Stigma Month is a campaign championed by the DC
20 Addiction and Recovery Coalition that aims to draw attention to burden of addiction stigma on
21 the District by combining stigma-reduction education, suicide prevention messaging, and
22 community engagement to raise awareness, reduce barriers to care and inspire action.
23
24 WHEREAS, substance use disorder is a chronic and treatable medical condition, yet
25 people with these disorders often face stigma and discrimination that can result in delayed
26 treatment, poor health outcomes, and reduced access to housing, employment, and community
27 support;
28
29 WHEREAS, individuals with substance use disorders disproportionately experience
30 housing and employment discrimination, with 64% of individuals endorsing the idea that
31 employers should be able to deny employment to people with a drug addiction, and 54% of in
32 individuals endorsing the idea that landlords should be allowed to deny housing to a person with
33 substance use disorder according to data from the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public
34 Health.
35
36 WHEREAS, according to the shatterproof Addiction Stigma index, a measurement tool
37 designed to assess attitudes about substance use and people who use substances, over 74% of
38 Americans do not view a substance use disorder as a chronic medical illness, and over 50%
39 believe that substance use disorders stem from bad character or lack of moral strength;
40
41 WHEREAS, data from the Shatterproof Addiction Stigma Index also reveals that
42 addiction stigma remains prevalent within provider communities with nearly 44% health care
43 professionals endorsing the harmful belief that medications for opioid use disorder merely
44 substitute one drug for another while 41% of the public expressed that same belief;

45
46 WHEREAS, for individuals with substance use disorders, addiction stigma creates an
47 additional barrier to treatment and recovery, In 2023, over 48 million Americans were diagnosed
48 with a substance use disorder only 4.5% of individuals received any form of treatment at a
49 facility according to the 2023 National Survey on Drug Use and Health (NSDUH);
50
51 WHEREAS, individuals with substance use disorders are at substantially increased risk
52 for suicidal ideation, suicide attempts, and suicide death compared with the general population;
53
54 WHEREAS, according to CDC and National Violent Death Reporting System data,
55 opioids are detected in approximately one-fifth of suicide decedents who undergo toxicology
56 testing, and alcohol is detected in approximately 35% to 40% of suicide decedents who undergo
57 toxicology testing;
58
59 WHEREAS, data from the DC Health Opioid Dashboard reveals that 51% of District
60 residents who died of a fatal opioid overdose had received mental health treatment and 34% had
61 received substance use treatment within 365 days of death;
62
63 WHEREAS, recent findings reveal that fatal opioid overdoses in the District are
64 declining for the first time in 10 years while non-fatal opioid overdoses are increasing
65 significantly;
66
67 WHEREAS, fatal opioid overdoses in the District disproportionately impact Black
68 communities, with Black residents accounting for 85% of opioid overdose deaths according to
69 the DC Health Opioid Dashboard;
70
71 WHEREAS, the dashboard also reveals that wards 7 and 8 had the highest rates if fatal
72 opioid overdose in the district over the past 12 months, at 40.1 and 55.1 deaths per 100,000
73 residents, respectively;
74
75 WHEREAS, FDA-approved medications such as methadone, long-acting injectable
76 buprenorphine, and extended-release naltrexone are safe and highly effective medications which
77 can cut the risk of overdose death in half;
78
79 WHEREAS, ending addiction stigma requires both changing public attitudes and
80 removing institutional and practical barriers that prevent residents from obtaining prevention,
81 harm reduction, treatment, housing, and recovery supports;
82
83 WHEREAS, a comprehensive District response requires adequate and sustained funding,
84 a skilled and supported workforce, accessible facilities and community services, and meaningful
85 participation by people with lives experience of substance use and recovery;
86
87 WHEREAS, the District must strengthen connections around amount emergency care,
88 stabilization, treatment, and community services, and expand housing and residential options so

89 that residents, including those with complex medical and behavioral health needs, have safe
90 places to live and consistent support;
91
92 IT IS HEREBY RESOLVED, BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF
93 COLUMBIA, that this resolution may be cited as the “DC End Addiction Stigma Month
94 Recognition Resolution of 2026”.
95
96 Sec 2. The Council of the District of Columbia recognizes the month of October, 2026 as
97 “DC End Addiction Stigma Month”.
98
99 Sec. 3. This resolution shall take effect immediately upon the first date of publication in
100 the District of Columbia Register.
