Dear Chairman Mendelson:
Enclosed for consideration and adoption by the Council of the District of Columbia is the Business
Organizations Amendment Act of 2026. The legislation would update the District of Columbia’s laws
for entity registration and filings to align with current practices and industry best practices. The
legislation also seeks to clarify areas of ambiguity, confirm the responsibilities for registered agents, and
standardizes entity filings where appropriate. The legislation also seeks to ensure that the District’s
business registration and entity process is consistent with current practices in light of the launch of the
Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection’s new Corporate Online System, which the Council of
the District of Columbia funded. The new system, which will require electronic filings, and registration
will go live in December 2026. These conforming amendments are necessary for the success of the new
system.
Below are a few highlights of the Business Organizations Amendment Act of 2026:
1. Requiring registered agents and those executing entity filings to be at least 18 years or age;
2. Making electronic filings the default;
3. Updating the entity filing and registration requirements;
4. Updating the trade name filing and registration requirements;
5. Standardizing the submission of records to the Mayor;
6. Clarifying when the Mayor can terminate the listings for commercial registered agents and
remove registered agents;
7. Allowing for electronic service of process; and
8. Clarifying the filing of a series of a statutory trust.
I encourage the Council to take immediate action to approve this legislation. If you have any questions
regarding this legislation, please contact Tiffany Crowe, Director, Department of Licensing and
Consumer Protection, at tiffany.crowe@dc.gov.
Sincerely,

Conclusion
Funds are sufficient in the fiscal year 2026 through fiscal year 2029 budget and financial plan to
implement the bill. The Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection (DLCP) can implement
the bill within current resources.
Background
Title 29 of the District of Columbia Code governs entities1, both domestic and foreign, that are formed
or conduct business in the District, providing the legal framework for operation and dissolution.
Entities must register with the DLCP’s Corporation Division, a process that is separate from, and in
advance of, an entity’s application for a business license, permit, tax registration and the like, within
the District.
The bill requires electronic filing, updates address rules, clarifies who can form certain business
entities, revises procedures for registered agents and dissolutions, updates rules for partners leaving
1 Defined as business corporations; nonprofit corporations; general partnerships; limited partnerships;
limited liability companies; general cooperative associations; limited cooperative associations;
unincorporated nonprofit associations; statutory, business, or common-law business trusts; or any other
person that has a legal existence separate from any interest holder of that person or that has the power to
acquire an interest in real property in its own name.

or merging limited liability partnerships, addresses limited terms of existence for cooperative
associations, and clarifies how to file a series within a statutory trust.
The bill makes technical changes to the code, which include:
• Modifying, updating or transferring existing definitions and adding new terms to the law.2
• Updating and adding references to electronic mail (e-mail) addresses and phone numbers as
methods for communication, notification, and required contact information.
• Repealing the 45-day period of Council review3 of the establishment or revision of fees4 by
the Mayor for entity filings, and for copying and certifying a copy of any entity filing.
• Standardizing the use of “submit” instead of “deliver” within the chapter and amended code.
• Requiring specific declarations submitted—when completing articles of domestication,
merger, or membership exchange—include a certified5 copy of the filing effectuating the
approval dated within 90 days of the date of submittal.
• Repealing Subchapter I-C of Title 47, governing Trade Names, and transferring that language
to Title 29, under a newly established Subchapter VIII.
• Amending code6 to allow one or more persons to incorporate7 in the District, instead of the
current five or more natural persons or two or more associations.
• Requiring an incorporator, organizer, or registered agent to be 18-years of age or older.
• Requiring general and limited cooperative associations that set a limited term of existence in
their respective articles of incorporation and organization, to address the terms of existence
prior to reaching the end of the term.
• Requiring the filing of a series of a statutory trust be in accordance with the filing of a series
of a limited liability company (LLC)8.
Current law9 defines permissible means of delivery to include delivery by hand, mail, commercial
delivery service, and electronic transmission, effective only upon receipt by the Mayor. The bill
makes electronic delivery the required method, unless prior approval is provided for another means,
and clarifies that documents are considered received when submitted to the Mayor.
Current law10 requires business entities11 to submit biennial reports to the Mayor. The bill expands
the disclosure guidelines to include the purpose of the entity; list beneficial owners and their contact

authorizes the Mayor to remove registered agents whose information is not current; allows service

registered agent’s listing if the entity is dissolved or not in good standing.
Current law12 allows the Mayor to commence administrative dissolution of a domestic filing entity
through service if the entity fails to satisfy requirements13 outlined in the law. The bill amends the
code to clarify that the notice state the grounds for dissolution and its effective date; specify that the
60-day cure period starts after the submission of notice, instead of the service of the notice; and
require the Mayor to notify the entity’s registered agent of the administrative dissolution.
Current law14 allows the Mayor to commence administrative revocation of a statement of
qualification of a limited liability partnership if the entity fails to satisfy requirements13 outlined in
law. The bill amends the code to clarify that the Mayor must submit notice, instead of serve notice;
and that if the grounds stated in the notice are not cured within the 60 day period, the Mayor shall
administratively revoke the statement of qualification, publish the notice online, and notify the
entity’s registered agent. Presently, the Mayor is required to file the statement and serve the
partnership.
Current law15 provides that an LLC is formed and effective when the Mayor files the certification of
organization and at least one person becomes a member. The bill specifies for a professional LLC
that only individuals licensed in the District can be members, managers, or perform the professional
work of the corporate entity; however, if the LLC has just one member, the secretary of the LLC
doesn’t require licensure, but any professional work cannot be performed without it.
Financial Plan Impact
Funds are sufficient in the fiscal year 2026 through fiscal year 2029 budget and financial plan to
implement the bill. The DLCP can implement the bill within current resources. Processes outlined in
the bill were built into the new CorpOnline System, which was launched by the DLCP in December
2025.
