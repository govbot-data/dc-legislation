Dear Secretary Howard:
Today, along with Councilmember Zachary Parker, I am introducing the “Unaccompanied Refugee Minor
Placement Amendment Act of 2026”. This legislation will clarify the D.C. Code to allow the District to provide
federally funded foster care and supportive services to more children who enter the country alone while
fleeing persecution or abuse. Over time, it will provide pathways to safe, loving homes, legal status, and
financial independence to many youth who might otherwise languish in group settings without appropriate
supports.
The federal Office of Refugee Resettlement (ORR) administers the Unaccompanied Refugee Minor (URM)
program to create a path out of institutional settings like shelters and group homes and into foster care for
the most vulnerable immigrant youth. Most children who enter the country by themselves have relatives
already living in the United States who can welcome them. ORR arranges to place these children with family
who can care for them while they participate in immigration proceedings. Thousands of youth each year,
however, do not have resident sponsors willing and able to take them in. These children can spend years
residing in congregate (group) care without access to legal counsel or robust supportive services. Many of
these children are deported back to dangerous conditions in their home countries. Those who remain are
not set up for success in the United States.
The URM program offers a potentially lifesaving alternative to this path. Through this initiative, children who
enter the country as refugees, asylees, victims of human trafficking, or in similar situations and who do not
have an available sponsor are eligible for alternative placements with intensive services. ORR places eligible
youth in foster care programs administered by the states and funded by the federal government. It also
provides English language training and educational support, job training, immigration legal counsel, and
family reunification services.
To administer the URM program in the District, ORR partners with the Department of Human Services’ Office
of Refugee Resettlement (DCORR). DCORR, in turn, contracts with the Child and Family Services Agency to
provide culturally and linguistically appropriate foster care. The day-to-day operation of the URM program is

overseen by Lutheran Social Services of the National Capital Area (LSS), which receives referrals directly from
ORR and works to arrange placements. Before a child can be placed in care, however, the Office of the
Attorney General (OAG) must petition D.C. Superior Court for a finding that the youth is a “neglected child”
under District law. In the District, children cannot remain legally separated from their parents without court
supervision of their ongoing placement through an active legal case. For that case to start and continue, OAG
must be able to represent to the Court that it believes a child has been abused or neglected.
Unfortunately, in some cases referred to LSS, District law provides OAG with no basis to file a neglect petition
because the District’s statutory definition of “neglected child” does not describe all youth referred through
the URM program. As a result, unless there is another statutory basis for a neglect finding, LSS must decline
a referral. In Fiscal Year 2026, the organization maintained 24 licensed foster care beds but was only able to
reach a maximum of 19 youth in care at one time. That year, LSS had to decline over 200 referrals.
If District law does not change to allow for more URM placements, LSS’s ability to provide care will continue
to degrade. Because of the number of referrals it has declined, its Fiscal Year 2027 funding was reduced to a
level that can support only 20 placements. This reduction has made it harder to achieve the economies of
scale necessary to operate the program efficiently, increasing the cost per placement and undermining the
long-term viability of LSS’s continued administration of the program. The URM program cannot sustain any
additional reductions. Statutory change is needed to optimize the District’s available placements and ensure
as many youth as possible can benefit from the District’s ORR partnership.
This legislation makes that change by amending the statutory definition of “neglected child” to include all
children referred through the URM program. This narrow additional basis for a finding of neglect will allow
LSS to support youth who are alone in the country and who may otherwise face years of congregate
conditions and potential deportation.
The bill also aligns District law with federal regulations governing the URM program. Federal law prohibits
contacting an unaccompanied minor’s parents in their native country because contact could be dangerous
to the parents. District law, however, requires parents to be provided notice of their children’s neglect cases.
This legislation makes a narrow exception to that requirement to ensure agencies, attorneys, and D.C.
Superior Court do not put family members at risk by attempting to contact them during legal proceedings.
I deeply appreciate the partnership of Councilmember Parker, LSS, OAG, DC Courts, Legal Aid D.C., and
Volunteer Legal Advocates in crafting this important legislation and look forward to providing more
vulnerable children safety and stability in the District. Should you have any questions about this legislation,
please contact my Committee Director Dan Passon at dpasson@dccouncil.gov.
Sincerely,

17 To amend Chapter 23 of Title 16 of the District of Columbia Code to allow for the foster care
18 placement, through the Office of Refugee Resettlement’s Unaccompanied Refugee Minor
19 (URM) program, of URMs who are not otherwise defined as “neglected children” and
20 to align District law with federal regulations prohibiting contact with the parents of
21 URMs in their native countries; and to amend the Prevention of Child Abuse and Neglect
22 Act of 1977 to prohibit any Child and Family Services Agency policy requiring a
23 diligent search for a URM’s parents, relatives, and significant non-relatives from
24 mandating contacts with persons and institutions in a URM’s country of origin that may
25 undermine parents’ safety.
26
27 BE IT ENACTED BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this
28 act may be cited as the “Unaccompanied Refugee Minor Placement Amendment Act of 2026”.

33 “Subchapter VIII. Special Considerations in Matters Involving Unaccompanied Refugee
34 Minors.

55 “Notwithstanding any other provision of law, in any proceeding under this chapter where

58 “(1) Serve notice on, or permit the service of notice on, the party residing in the

61 “(2) In a neglect case, waive service of the party residing in the child’s country of
62 origin.”.
63 Sec. 3. Title III of the Prevention of Child Abuse and Neglect Act of 1977, effective
64 September 23, 1977 (D.C. Law 2-22; D.C. Official Code § 4-1303.01 et seq.), is amended by
65 adding a new section 313a to read as follows:
66 “Sec. 313a. Restriction on diligent search policy for unaccompanied refugee minors.
67 “Any Agency policy requiring a diligent search to locate a child’s parents, relatives, and
68 significant non-relatives whose identities or whereabouts are unknown may not require direct
69 outreach to parties or institutions located in the child’s country of origin if the affected child is an
70 unaccompanied refugee minor under section 16-2301(40a) of the District of Columbia Official
71 Code.”.
