ENROLLED ORIGINAL
A RESOLUTION
26-512
IN THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA
September 22, 2026
To declare the existence of an emergency with respect to the need to amend Title 47 of the
District of Columbia Official Code to clarify the applicability of the motor vehicle rental
sales tax rate.
RESOLVED, BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this
resolution may be cited as the “Peer-to-Peer Car Sharing Rental Vehicle Sales Tax Clarification
Emergency Declaration Resolution of 2026”.
Sec. 2. (a) Under current law, the Office of Tax and Revenue collects the rental vehicle
sales tax of 9.25% on peer-to-peer car sharing services transactions when the owner has more
than 5 vehicles. That is the case as a result of amendments to Title 47 of the District of Columbia
Official Code made by the Internet Sales Tax Amendment Act of 2018, effective March 22, 2019
(D.C. Law 22-258; 66 DCR 1362), that require all marketplace facilitators to collect and pay
District sales tax on transactions that occur on their platforms. However, the code provisions
imposing the rental vehicle sales tax rate do not explicitly mention peer-to-peer car sharing
services, creating confusion among the public. To provide clarity, the Fiscal Year 2027 Budget
Support Act of 2026, enacted on August 14, 2026 (D.C. Act 26-418; 73 DCR 11735) (“Fiscal
Year 2027 Budget Support Act”), included a technical amendment in subtitle 7-B adding
language to D.C. Official Code §§ 47-2002(a) and 47-2202(a) making it clear that the 9.25%
(and, as of October 1, 2026, 11%) rental vehicle sales tax applies “to the sale or charges from
any transactions for the sharing of a vehicle or utility trailer made through a marketplace as
defined in § 47-2001(g-4), including a peer-to-peer sharing program” and that this tax applies
“regardless of whether the rental vehicle or utility trailer is owned by a rental operator as defined
in § 50-1505.01(6) or part of a rental fleet as defined in § 50-1505.01(5).”
(b) According to the Office of Tax and Revenue, the language in the Fiscal Year 2027
Budget Support Act would apply the rental vehicle sales tax rate to all peer-to-peer car-sharing
service transactions, regardless of whether the owner has fewer than 5 vehicles. This would
create a disparity between peer-to-peer car-sharing service transactions and traditional rental
vehicle services, which are taxed only at the rental vehicle sales tax rate if the owner has 5 or
more rental vehicles. That disparity will negatively affect peer-to-peer car-sharing services in the
District.

(c) The Fiscal Year 2027 Budget Support Act has not yet completed congressional
review; however, provisions of the Fiscal Year 2027 Budget Support Emergency Act of 2026,
effective August 13, 2026 (D.C. Act 26-416; 73 DCR 11509), that are identical to those in the
Fiscal Year 2027 Budget Support Act, are already effective and will apply as of October 1, 2026.
As such, it is necessary to move an emergency amendment to both Title 47 of the District of
Columbia Official Code and to the Fiscal Year 2027 Budget Support Act to revert to the prior
language so that the new motor vehicle rental sales tax rate of 11% only applies to peer-to-peer
car sharing transactions when the owner of the vehicle has 5 or more vehicles available for
sharing in the District.
Sec. 3. The Council of the District of Columbia determines that the circumstances
enumerated in section 2 constitute emergency circumstances making it necessary that the Peer-
to-Peer Car Sharing Rental Vehicle Sales Tax Clarification Emergency Amendment Act of 2026
be adopted after a single reading.
Sec. 4. This resolution shall take effect immediately.
