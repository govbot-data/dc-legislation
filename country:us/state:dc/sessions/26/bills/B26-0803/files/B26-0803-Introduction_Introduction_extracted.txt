RE: Request for introduction of the Uniform Assignment for Benefit of Creditors Act
of 2026.
Dear Chairman Mendelson:
Pursuant to Rule 401(b)(1) of the Rules of Organization and Procedure for the
Council, this is to request, on behalf of the District of Columbia Uniform Law
Commission, that you introduce the proposed “Uniform Assignment for Benefit of
Creditors Act of 2026.”
Under the uniform act, a financially distressed business, an assignor, may transfer
control of its assets to an assignee, who acts as a fiduciary for the business’s creditors.
The assignee may sell the business’s assets and distribute the proceeds to the business’s
creditors. These proceeds go towards payment of the creditors’ claims against the
assignor. The Act creates an alternative to other procedures available to the assignor for
winding up its business, and provides benefits that are not available through a federal
bankruptcy case or a state or federal law receivership. An assignment under this act
generally will be more flexible, quicker, and less costly than the other alternatives. It is an
improvement upon these alternatives in five significant ways: (1) it is debtor-initiated; (2)
it does not require judicial supervision; (3) it provides assurances to creditors by
imposing fiduciary duties upon the assignee; (4) it is designed to maximize the value of
the business’s assets for the benefit of all creditors; and (5) it encourages cooperation
between the distressed business and the creditors by aligning these parties’ goals.
The uniform act would replace a rudimentary 1964 Act of Congress with a
detailed, state-of-the-art uniform act, developed based on many decades of experience
with these transactions. In addition, the uniform act is compatible with the recently
enacted Uniform Commercial Receivership Act, D.C. Law 25-269.

The uniform act was completed by the National Conference of Commissioners on
Uniform State Laws only last year in 2025 and already has been enacted, thus far, in six
states (Alabama, Arizona, Delaware, Iowa, Nebraska, and Utah) and introduced in three
more states (Colorado, Oklahoma, and West Virginia).
A proposed “Uniform Assignment for Benefit of Creditors Act of 2026” is being
filed with this letter. In addition, the following documents have been filed: (1) a
summary of the Uniform Act; (2) a statement as to why the Uniform Act should be
adopted; and (3) the official version of the Uniform Assignment for Benefit of Creditors
Act with comments.
I would be pleased to answer any questions and to provide any additional
information requested.

187 “§ 28-2204. Requirements for assignee and assignment agreement.
188 “(a) An assignee must be a person that:
189 “(1) Is not a creditor, affiliate, or insider of the assignor;
190 “(2) Is not an affiliate or insider of a creditor of the assignor;
191 “(3) Does not have a claim against the assignment estate, other than a claim for
192 fees and expenses to be paid under the assignment agreement;
193 “(4) Does not have a material financial interest in the outcome of the assignment,
194 other than a claim for fees and expenses to be paid under the assignment agreement;
195 “(5) Does not hold an equity interest in the assignor other than a noncontrolling
196 interest in a publicly traded company; and
197 “(6) Is not an affiliate of a person that fails to satisfy paragraph (1), (2), (3), (4), or
8

198 (5).
199 “(b) A person that satisfies subsection (a) is not precluded from being an assignee merely
200 because the person performed services for the assignor before the assignment.
201 “(c) An assignment agreement must be in a record signed by the assignor and the
202 assignee. The record must:
203 “(1) State the name and address of the assignor and of the assignee;
204 “(2) Transfer or provide for a transfer of all the assignor’s assets;
205 “(3) Describe the assigned assets in sufficient detail to identify the assets;
206 “(4) Provide for the distribution of the assignment estate;
207 “(5) Describe the fees to be charged by the assignee in connection with the
208 assignment, including the basis on which they are to be calculated; and
209 “(6) Include a representation by the assignor, under penalty of perjury, that the
210 assignor is assigning all the assignor’s assets.
211 “(d) If an assignee relies in good faith on the assignor’s representation made under
212 subsection (c)(6), all the assignor’s assets are deemed to be assigned, even if the representation is
213 inaccurate.
214 “§ 28-2205. Effect of assignment; when assignment agreement effective.
215 “(a) An assignee obtains the rights, title, and interests of the assignor in the assigned
216 assets.
217 “(b) If the assignor is an organization, an assignee obtains the rights, title, and interests of
218 the assignor in assets acquired after the assignment.
219 “(c) Except as provided in § 28-2210(b)(12), an assignee takes each assigned asset
220 subject to an existing interest in the asset held by another person.

223 “(e) An assignment is subject to other law under which the assignment may be fraudulent
224 or otherwise voidable.
225 “(f) The effective date of an assignment agreement is the date the agreement is signed by
226 the last party to the agreement that is required to sign the agreement unless a later date is
227 identified in the agreement as the effective date.

257 “§ 28-2207. Notification to creditors.
258 “(a) Unless a creditor waives in a signed record the right to notification, an assignee shall
259 send a notification of the assignment to each creditor known to the assignee within a reasonable
260 time not to exceed 30 days after the effective date of the assignment agreement.
261 “(b) The notification must:
262 “(1) Be in a record signed by the assignee;
263 “(2) Include the assignee’s name, address, and other contact information
264 reasonably necessary to communicate with the assignee;
265 “(3) Provide reasonable instructions for submitting a proof of claim using the
266 method established by the assignee under § 28-2209(b)(5); and
11

267 “(4) Identify the date established under § 28-2209(b)(6) by which each creditor
268 whose claim is not otherwise allowed without a timely proof of claim under this chapter must
269 submit a proof of claim.
270 “(c) An assignee shall use reasonable means to provide the information in subsection (b)
271 to unknown creditors, including by any means the assignor regularly used to:
272 “(1) Provide information to the assignor’s creditors; or
273 “(2) Communicate information about the assignor, other than advertising, to the
274 public.

289 “(5) If the assignment includes a legal or equitable interest in real property or
12

290 titled personal property, cooperate with the assignee in taking actions under § 28-2206;

293 “(A) A list of all assets;
294 “(B) A list of all the assignor’s employees, including those whose
295 employment is terminated in connection with the assignment; and
296 “(C) A list of all the assignor’s known creditors, including, for each

299 “(7) Verify under penalty of perjury the accuracy of the lists required under

301 “(8) With respect to a legal or equitable interest in property restricted from

17

409 “(d) A creditor’s claim is allowed if the creditor succeeds in a dispute under § 28-

424 “§ 28-2212. Disputed and disallowed claims.
425 “(a) An assignee may dispute a creditor’s claim before final distribution by sending
426 notification in a record stating the nature of the assignee’s dispute to the creditor.
427 “(b) If a dispute cannot be resolved consensually, the assignee may commence a
18

428 proceeding under § 28-2221 to disallow the claim. The assignee must commence the proceeding
429 before final distribution under § 28-2215. If the proceeding is not filed before final distribution,
430 the assignee shall allow the claim under § 28-2211.
431 “(c) An assignee shall create a dollar-for-dollar reserve for the estimated amount of the
432 potential distribution on a disputed claim.
433 “(d) Subject to subsection (b), an assignee shall disallow a claim for reimbursement or
434 contribution of a person that is liable with the assignor on, or that has secured, the claim, to the
435 extent:
436 “(1) The claim against the assignment estate is disallowed;
437 “(2) The claim for reimbursement or contribution is contingent as of the time of
438 allowance or disallowance; or
439 “(3) The person asserts a right of subrogation to the rights of a creditor.
440 “(e) A claim for reimbursement or contribution of a person liable with the assignor on, or
441 that has secured, the claim that becomes fixed after the effective date of the assignment
442 agreement must be determined, and must be allowed or disallowed, subject to subsection (b), as
443 if the claim had become fixed before the effective date of the assignment agreement.
444 “(f) An assignee may reconsider the assignee’s decision to allow or disallow a claim for
445 cause. If a reconsidered claim is allowed under § 28-2211, before the assignee makes additional
446 payments or transfers to other creditors that are equal or junior in priority under § 28-2215 to the
447 reconsidered claim, the creditor with the reconsidered claim must receive a payment or transfer
448 in an amount proportionate in value to the payments or transfers already received by the other
449 creditors. This subsection does not modify the assignee’s right under other law to recover from a
450 creditor an excess payment or transfer made to the creditor. If a reconsidered claim is disallowed,
19

451 the assignee shall comply with subsections (b) and (c).
452 “§ 28-2213. Proof of claim.
453 “(a) A proof of claim must:
454 “(1) State the name, address, and other contact information reasonably necessary
455 to communicate with the creditor;
456 “(2) State the amount of the claim;
457 “(3) Briefly state the nature of the claim;
458 “(4) Identify any asset of the assignment estate securing the claim;
459 “(5) Be signed by the creditor under penalty of perjury;
460 “(6) Include a copy of a record, if any, on which the claim is based;
461 “(7) Be submitted using the method established under § 28-2209(b)(5); and
462 “(8) Be submitted by the date established by the assignee under § 28-2209(b)(6).
463 “(b) A proof of claim submitted in compliance with this section is prima facie evidence
464 of the validity and amount of the claim.
465 “(c) The submission by a creditor of a proof of claim in compliance with this section
466 constitutes the creditor’s:
467 “(1) Consent to the jurisdiction of the court under § 28-2221; and
468 “(2) Assignment to the assignee of any right of the creditor to bring a voidable
469 transaction action relating to the creditor’s claim.
470 “§ 28-2214. Rights of transferees.
471 “(a) An assignee’s disposition of an asset:
472 “(1) Transfers to a transferee for value all of the assignee’s rights in the asset;
473 “(2) Discharges the assignee’s lien and, to the extent the assignment creates a
20

474 security interest in favor of the assignee, the assignee’s security interest; and
475 “(3) Discharges any subordinate security interest or other lien subordinate to the
476 assignee’s lien.
477 “(b) A transferee that acts in good faith takes free of the rights and interests described in
478 subsection (a), even if the assignee fails to comply with this chapter or the requirements of a
479 judicial proceeding.
480 “(c) If a transferee does not take free of the rights and interests described in subsection
481 (a), the transferee takes the asset subject to:
482 “(1) The assignee’s rights in the assets of the assignment estate;
483 “(2) The assignee’s lien and, if applicable, security interest; and
484 “(3) Any other security interest or other lien.
485 “(d) Unless otherwise provided in a record, any warranty arising by operation of other
486 law is disclaimed to the extent permitted by other law.
487 “(e) If a subordinate security interest or other lien is discharged under this section, the
488 assignee may file a record with the official or office responsible for maintaining an official filing,
489 recording, registration, or certificate-of-title system covering the asset secured by the security
490 interest or other lien. The record must state that the security interest or other lien is discharged as
491 a subordinate security interest or other lien in connection with a disposition under an assignment
492 for the benefit of creditors of the assignor whose asset is subject to the security interest or other
493 lien.
494 “§ 28-2215. Distributions.
495 “(a) In this section, “protected secured creditor” means a secured creditor whose lien:
496 “(1) Is a perfected lien;
21

497 “(2) Cannot be avoided by the assignee under § 28-2210(b)(12); and
498 “(3) Is not subordinate to the assignee’s lien.
499 “(b) Except as provided in § 28-2216, the assignee shall pay claims from the assignment
500 estate allowed under § 28-2211 in the order of priority stated in this section.
501 “(c) Unless otherwise agreed between the assignee and a protected secured creditor,
502 before distributions under subsections (d), (e), (f), and (g), and in accordance with the priorities
503 of creditors with liens under other law, the protected secured creditor shall receive the asset or
504 the proceeds from the collection on or disposition of the asset to the extent of the value of the
505 protected secured creditor’s interest in the asset, less the assignee’s reasonable and necessary
506 expenses of preserving or disposing of the asset to the extent the expenses benefit the protected
507 secured creditor and are incurred with the protected secured creditor’s consent or acquiescence.
508 The protected secured creditor has an unsecured claim under subsection (g)(2) for the amount of
509 the claim that remains after deducting the amount or value of an asset the protected secured
510 creditor receives under this subsection. To the extent a claim is secured by an asset the value of
511 which, after the deductions provided under this subsection, is greater than the amount of the
512 claim, the protected secured creditor may receive interest on the claim and any reasonable fees,
513 costs, or charges provided for under the agreement or other law under which the claim arose.
514 “(d) After the distributions under subsection (c), the assignee shall pay the necessary
515 costs of the administration of the assignment estate. The costs include:
516 “(1) Fees and reimbursements of the expenses of the assignee and any
517 professionals engaged by the assignee;
518 “(2) Post-assignment taxes incurred by the assignee;
519 “(3) Post-assignment rent incurred by the assignee in occupying premises on
22

520 which assets of the assignment estate are located or the business of the assignor is conducted;
521 “(4) Post-assignment lease payments incurred by the assignee in renting personal
522 property used in the business of the assignor; and
523 “(5) Amounts required to be paid under the assignment agreement for expenses of
524 winding up the assignment under § 28-2219.
525 “(e) After the distributions under subsections (c) and (d), the assignee shall pay claims
526 entitled to priority under federal law including under 31 U.S.C. Section 3713 from the
527 assignment estate.
528 “(f) After the distributions under subsections (c), (d), and (e), the assignee shall pay
529 claims from the assignment estate for wages, salaries, or commissions earned not more than 180
530 days before the earlier of the effective date of the assignment agreement or the cessation of the
531 assignor’s business. Payment is limited to the greater of:
532 “(1) The amount of the claim allowed as a priority claim ahead of claims of other
533 unsecured creditors under the Bankruptcy Code, 11 U.S.C. Section 101 et seq.; or
534 “(2) The amount allowed as a priority claim ahead of claims of other unsecured
535 creditors under applicable non-bankruptcy law.
536 “(g) After the distributions under subsections (c), (d), (e), and (f), each creditor shall
537 receive a distribution of the assets of the assignment estate in the following order of priority:
538 “(1) Unsecured claims entitled to priority ahead of claims of other unsecured
539 creditors under other law; and
540 “(2) Unsecured claims not entitled to priority.
541 “(h) If the assets available for distribution to claims with equal priority under subsection
542 (g) are insufficient to pay the total amount of the claims with that priority, each creditor with a
23

543 claim with that priority shall receive a pro rata distribution of the available assets based on the
544 proportion the amount of the creditor’s claim bears to the total amount of the claims with that
545 priority.
546 “(i) If the claims entitled to the distribution under subsections (c), (d), (e), (f), and (g) are
547 paid in full, the residue shall be distributed to allowed claims evidenced by a late-filed proof of
548 claim, other than a late-filed claim allowed by the assignee under § 28-2211(b)(2), and, after the
549 allowed claims evidenced by a late-filed proof of claim have been paid in full, as provided in the
550 assignment agreement.
551 “(j) An assignee may make interim distributions after considering future expenses and the
552 reserves for disputed claims established under § 28-2212(c).
553 “§ 28-2216. Claim subordination.
554 “(a) A subordination agreement is enforceable under this chapter to the same extent the
555 agreement is enforceable under other law.
556 “(b) Subject to subsection (c), the following claims are subordinate to a claim or interest
557 that is senior or equal in priority to a claim or interest represented by a security or other equity
558 interest in the assignor or an affiliate of the assignor:
559 “(1) A claim arising from rescission of a purchase or sale of the security or other
560 equity interest;
561 “(2) A claim for damages arising from the purchase or sale of the security or other
562 equity interest; and
563 “(3) A claim for reimbursement or contribution allowed on account of the
564 rescission or damage claim.
565 “(c) If the security is common stock or another common equity interest, a claim subject to
24

566 subordination under subsection (b) has the same priority as common stock or another common
567 equity interest.
568 “§ 28-2217. Liability.
569 “(a) An assignor is not personally liable for an act or omission by the assignee.
570 “(b) An assignee is not personally liable for an act or omission by the assignor.
571 “(c) A representative designated by an assignor under § 28-2208(b)(4) is exculpated to
572 the same extent as a person acting on behalf of the assignor under other law had there been no
573 assignment, except for an act or omission resulting from the representative’s gross negligence or
574 willful misconduct.
575 “(d) A term of an assignment agreement relieving the assignee of liability is
576 unenforceable to the extent the agreement relieves the assignee of liability for an act or omission
577 committed in bad faith or with reckless indifference to the purposes of the assignment or the
578 interests of the creditors of the assignment estate.
579 “(e) Subject to subsection (f), an assignee is personally liable for breach of a fiduciary
580 duty under § 28-2209(a). If the assignee is liable:
581 “(1) The assignee is personally liable to a creditor for an individualized harm to
582 the creditor if the harm is not shared by all creditors or a class of creditors; and
583 “(2) The assignee is personally liable to the assignment estate for a harm shared
584 by all creditors or a class of creditors.
585 “(f) An assignee is not liable if, in the performance of the assignee’s duties and exercise
586 of the assignee’s powers, the assignee relies in good faith on:
587 “(1) A record of the assignor;
588 “(2) Information, an opinion, a report, or a statement presented to the assignee by
25

589 the assignor’s officer or employee, a committee of the assignor’s board of directors, an
590 independent director or manager of the assignor, or another representative of the assignor; or
591 “(3) Information, an opinion, a report, or a statement presented to the assignee by
592 another person that has been selected with reasonable care by or on behalf of the assignee as to a
593 matter the assignee reasonably believes is within the other person’s professional or expert
594 competence.
595 “§ 28-2218. Assignee removal; successor assignee.
596 “(a) The assignor or a creditor may request the Superior Court of the District of Columbia
597 to remove the assignee, if the assignor or creditor has a reasonable belief grounds for removal
598 exist under subsection (b).
599 “(b) After a request under subsection (a) or on the court’s initiative in an action pending
600 before the court under § 28-2221, the court may remove an assignee:
601 “(1) For cause, including the assignee’s fraud, dishonesty, incompetence, gross
602 mismanagement, or failure to comply with this chapter; or
603 “(2) If removal of the assignee best serves the interests of the creditors.
604 “(c) After an assignee resigns, or is removed, dies, or becomes incapacitated, a successor
605 assignee provided for in the assignment agreement becomes the assignee, unless the successor
606 assignee is not eligible to be an assignee under § 28-2204(a) or is subject to removal under
607 subsection (b). A court shall appoint a successor assignee if:
608 “(1) The assignment agreement does not provide for a successor assignee; or
609 “(2) The successor assignee provided for in the assignment agreement is ineligible
610 to be an assignee under § 28-2204(a) or is subject to removal under subsection (b).
611 “(d) Subject to § 28-2217, an assignee that resigns, or is removed, dies, or becomes
26

612 incapacitated, is discharged from the assignee’s duties under this chapter when the assignee, or a

616 “(2) Submits to creditors a report summarizing the receipts and disbursements

618 “(e) Subject to an applicable privilege, a court may order an attorney, accountant, or other
619 person that has information in a record relating to the assignment estate or the assignor’s

635 administration of the assignment estate and the distributions under § 28-2215; and

643 “§ 28-2220. Interstate matters.

650 state.
651 “(c) If an assignee determines that a creditor should receive the treatment the creditor
652 would receive under an assignment made under the law of another state, the assignee may treat
