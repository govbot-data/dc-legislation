ENROLLED ORIGINAL
A CEREMONIAL RESOLUTION
26-230
IN THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA
October 20, 2026
To proclaim the Month of October, 2026 as "DC End Addiction Stigma Month" to raise
awareness of the ways addiction stigma can obstruct access to care and to ensure that
individuals with substance use disorder can reach respectful treatment and recovery
support;
WHEREAS, DC Ends Addiction Stigma Month is a campaign championed by the DC
Addiction and Recovery Coalition that aims to draw attention to burden of addiction stigma on
the District by combining stigma-reduction education, suicide prevention messaging, and
community engagement to raise awareness, reduce barriers to care and inspire action.
WHEREAS, substance use disorder is a chronic and treatable medical condition, yet
people with these disorders often face stigma and discrimination that can result in delayed
treatment, poor health outcomes, and reduced access to housing, employment, and community
support;
WHEREAS, individuals with substance use disorders disproportionately experience
housing and employment discrimination, with 64% of individuals endorsing the idea that
employers should be able to deny employment to people with a drug addiction, and 54% of in
individuals endorsing the idea that landlords should be allowed to deny housing to a person with
substance use disorder according to data from the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public
Health.
WHEREAS, according to the shatterproof Addiction Stigma index, a measurement tool
designed to assess attitudes about substance use and people who use substances, over 74% of
Americans do not view a substance use disorder as a chronic medical illness, and over 50%
believe that substance use disorders stem from bad character or lack of moral strength;
WHEREAS, data from the Shatterproof Addiction Stigma Index also reveals that
addiction stigma remains prevalent within provider communities with nearly 44% health care
professionals endorsing the harmful belief that medications for opioid use disorder merely
substitute one drug for another while 41% of the public expressed that same belief;

WHEREAS, for individuals with substance use disorders, addiction stigma creates an
additional barrier to treatment and recovery, In 2023, over 48 million Americans were diagnosed
with a substance use disorder only 4.5% of individuals received any form of treatment at a
facility according to the 2023 National Survey on Drug Use and Health (NSDUH);
WHEREAS, individuals with substance use disorders are at substantially increased risk
for suicidal ideation, suicide attempts, and suicide death compared with the general population;
WHEREAS, according to CDC and National Violent Death Reporting System data,
opioids are detected in approximately one-fifth of suicide decedents who undergo toxicology
testing, and alcohol is detected in approximately 35% to 40% of suicide decedents who undergo
toxicology testing;
WHEREAS, data from the DC Health Opioid Dashboard reveals that 51% of District
residents who died of a fatal opioid overdose had received mental health treatment and 34% had
received substance use treatment within 365 days of death;
WHEREAS, recent findings reveal that fatal opioid overdoses in the District are
declining for the first time in 10 years while non-fatal opioid overdoses are increasing
significantly;
WHEREAS, fatal opioid overdoses in the District disproportionately impact Black
communities, with Black residents accounting for 85% of opioid overdose deaths according to
the DC Health Opioid Dashboard.
WHEREAS, the dashboard also reveals that wards 7 and 8 had the highest rates if fatal
opioid overdose in the district over the past 12 months, at 40.1 and 55.1 deaths per 100,000
residents, respectively;
WHEREAS, FDA-approved medications such as methadone, long-acting injectable
buprenorphine, and extended-release naltrexone are safe and highly effective medications which
can cut the risk of overdose death in half;
WHEREAS ending addiction stigma requires both changing public attitudes and
removing institutional and practical barriers that prevent residents from obtaining prevention,
harm reduction, treatment, housing, and recovery supports;
WHEREAS, a comprehensive District response requires adequate and sustained funding,
a skilled and supported workforce, accessible facilities and community services, and meaningful
participation by people with lives experience of substance use and recovery;

ENROLLED ORIGINAL
WHEREAS, the District must strengthen connections around emergency care,
stabilization, treatment, and community services, and expand housing and residential options so
that residents, including those with complex medical and behavioral health needs, have safe
places to live and consistent support.
IT IS HEREBY RESOLVED, BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF
COLUMBIA, That this resolution may be cited as the “DC End Addiction Stigma Month
Recognition Resolution of 2026”.
Sec 2. The Council of the District of Columbia recognizes the month of October, 2026 as
“DC End Addiction Stigma Month”.
Sec. 3. This resolution shall take effect immediately.
