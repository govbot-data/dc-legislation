Today, along with Councilmembers Anita Bonds, Janeese Lewis George, Charles Allen, and Elissa Silverman, I
am introducing the “Green Workforce Pilot Amendment Act of 2026”. Recent changes to federal law require
the District to establish expanded work requirements for public benefits programs that will limit the reach of
our safety net. This legislation preserves residents’ access to benefits while connecting them with pathways
to independence. It establishes a Green Workforce Pilot Program that will provide residents with sufficient
hours of employment to meet federal work requirements and retain their benefits, meaningful work
experience, and support from job coaches to transition to full-time employment. Program participants will
serve the District by conducting stormwater maintenance, combating invasive plants, improving our
sustainable energy infrastructure, and otherwise working to conserve the environment. The theory behind
the program is that, for some residents, merely securing a job paired with supports will help them progress
in their pursuit of longer-term opportunities.
This pilot models a new approach in workforce development, prioritizing immediate access to work while
providing job experience and support that can set residents up for lasting financial independence. The
program will simultaneously empower residents to access the federal and District Earned Income Tax Credit,
providing them with a significant increase in income. Critically, in a time of limited District resources, the pilot
will rely on existing effective programs within the Department of Energy and Environment (DOEE), with the
Department of Small and Local Business Development’s Clean Teams, and with employers willing to train
residents and help them transition to full-time jobs.
Recent federal legislation created new Medicaid community engagement requirements and modified
Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) work requirements for able-bodied adults without
dependents (ABAWD). The federal government eliminated the District’s long-standing SNAP work
requirement waiver for ABAWDs and categorized more people as ABAWD, including people ages 56 to 60
and people with children ages 14 to 18. As a result, the Department of Human Services (DHS) estimates that
between 8,500 and 12,900 residents will lose access to SNAP. The Department of Health Care Finance (DHCF),

While many recipients of public benefits already work,2 the District must do a better job of creating
sustainable employment opportunities for those who do not. DHS currently invests in workforce and training
programs for SNAP and Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF) participants, but there are
significant opportunities for these programs to improve their employment and education outcomes. For
example, while DHS has made efforts to improve workforce training, outcomes from Fiscal Year 2025 – during
which only 10 percent of participants in the SNAP Employment and Training Program started new jobs–3
highlight room for growth and the need for more specialized models like this pilot.
The Green Workforce Pilot offers an opportunity for DHS to proactively partner with agencies and workforce
development programs that are already realizing positive employment outcomes, often by directly
employing individuals. This legislation requires DHS to establish a pilot program that provides residents with
part-time employment, with wages and hours tailored to avoiding benefits cliffs while allowing residents to
engage in training and job search activities, child care, and other duties as they prepare to transition to full-
time work. The pilot would employ residents in conservation and environmental roles that allow them to
earn income while making a tangible impact on their communities. The program would provide participants
with work experience and the coaching needed to pursue full-time employment.
Providing individuals with minimum wage jobs for between eight and 12 hours per week can bring them into
compliance with Medicaid and SNAP work requirements: participants will be exempt from SNAP work
requirements if they earn wages at least equal to the federal minimum wage multiplied by 30 hours ($870
per month) and will meet Medicaid community engagement requirements if they earn at least $580 a
month.4 Part-time work tailored to providing the minimum wages and hours necessary to sustain benefits,
coupled with the federal and local Earned Income Tax Credit, will allow families to grow their income while
pursuing a permanent job search and maintaining personal responsibilities.

28 Sec. 2. Establishment of Green Workforce Pilot Program.
29 (a) The Department of Human Services (the “Department”) shall establish, through its
30 Temporary Assistance for Needy Families Employment and Education Program, the Green
31 Workforce Pilot Program (the “Pilot”), to assist individuals with fulfilling Supplemental Nutrition
32 Assistance Program (“SNAP”) work requirements and Medicaid community engagement
33 requirements while gaining meaningful work experience, conserving the environment, and
34 improving the District’s physical infrastructure.
35 (b) The Pilot shall provide 250 people at a time with employment paying no less per hour
36 than the District minimum wage for no less than 32 hours per month and up to 48 hours per month.
1

37 The Pilot may employ individuals through a variety of programs and public- and private-sector
38 employers, including:
39 (1) Department of Small and Local Business Development Clean Teams;
40 (2) The DC Sustainable Energy Utility Workforce Development program;
41 (3) Green Pathways;
42 (4) Programs that control invasive plants; or
43 (5) The Green Stormwater Infrastructure Maintenance Program.
44 (c) The Pilot shall only enroll individuals:
45 (1) Not in compliance with SNAP able bodied adults without dependents
46 (ABAWD) work requirements, but otherwise eligible for SNAP; or
47 (2) Not in compliance with Medicaid community engagement requirements, but
48 otherwise eligible for Medicaid.
49 (d) Pilot participants shall begin work on Pilot initiatives no later than April 1, 2028.
50 (e) Each Pilot participant shall have access to job coaching services, which may be
51 provided through an existing District program.

53 Department shall collect any information associated with Pilot participation that verifies

58 engagement requirements.
