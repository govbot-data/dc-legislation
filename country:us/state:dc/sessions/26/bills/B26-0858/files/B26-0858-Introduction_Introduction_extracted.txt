Statement of Introduction
Restricting Editing and Alterations in Listing Photos Amendment Act of 2026
October 9, 2026
Today I am introducing the Restricting Editing and Alterations in Listing Photos Amendment
Act of 2026 with Councilmembers Matthew Frumin, Brianne K. Nadeau, Anita Bonds, Trayon
White, Janeese Lewis George, and Zachary Parker. This bill would increase transparency in real
property listings by requiring listing agents, realtors, and property managers to provide truthful
and accurate property descriptions and disclose the use of digital tools, including artificial
intelligence, to alter listing photos for properties offered for lease or sale in the District.
This bill would amend the Consumer Protections Procedures Act to make the use of digitally
altered photos in real property advertisements and listings a deceptive trade practice unless the
advertisement or listing contains a clear and conspicuous notice that the images used are digitally
altered, includes a written description of the material differences between the actual property and
any digitally altered photos, and includes a link to photos of the property that are not digitally
altered. It also amends D.C. Code § 47-2853.197 to clarify that the prohibition on real estate
agents, listing agents, and property managers falsely advertising real property includes the new
deceptive trade practice established by the bill.
The use of artificial intelligence in photo editing is pervasive in the rental and homebuying
industries. The practice itself is not inherently problematic, as it can save costs on staging
furniture and professional photography. However, digital editing tools can and do add fake
architectural features, change finishes, fixtures and appliances, and change views through
windows. Digitally altered photos are common in home and apartment listings in the District.
Some listings, particularly stock listing photos of generic rooms in apartment buildings, clearly
misrepresent window views and unit size.
Digitally altered photographs can cause prospective tenants and buyers to form inaccurate
expectations about a property, and those who rent or purchase sight unseen may not realize they
have been misled until after they have committed to the transaction and their options are limited.
New York City is considering rulemaking for similar restrictions and California recently passed
Assembly Bill 723 to require disclosure of digitally altered photos. District residents deserve to
know what they are actually getting when they look for a home, and this bill will make sure they
can trust that what they see in a listing is what they will find when they walk through the door.

30 To amend Chapter 39 of Title 28 of the District of Columbia Official Code to restrict the use
31 of digitally altered images in a real property advertisement or listing; and to amend
32 Chapter 28 of Title 47 of the District of Columbia Official Code to clarify that using a
33 digitally altered image in a real property advertisement is a prohibited act.
34
35 BE IT ENACTED BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That
36 this act may be cited as the “Restricting Editing and Alterations in Listing Photos Amendment
37 Act of 2026”.
38 Sec. 2. Chapter 39 of Title 28 of the District of Columbia Official Code is amended as
39 follows:
1

40 (a) Section 28-3901 is amended by adding a new paragraph (16) to read as follows:
41 “(16) “Digitally altered image” means an image that has been altered through the use
42 of photo editing software or artificial intelligence to add, remove, or change elements in an
43 image, including to the fixtures, furniture, appliances, flooring, walls, paint color, hardscape,
44 landscape, facade, floor plans, of a real property or to the elements outside of, or visible from,
45 a real property, including streetlights, utility poles, views through windows, and neighboring
46 properties. The term “digitally altered image” does not include an image where common photo
47 editing adjustments such as lighting, sharpening, white balance, color correction, angle,
48 straightening, cropping, or exposure, are made that do not materially change the representation
49 of the real property.”.
50 (b) Section 28-3904 is amended as follows:
51 (1) Subsection (ll) is amended by striking the phrase “; or” and inserting a
52 semicolon in its place.
53 (2) Subsection (mm) is amended by striking the period and inserting the phrase
54 “; or” in its place.
55 (3) A new subsection (nn) is added to read as follows:
56 “(nn) Fail to include a clear and conspicuous disclosure in an advertisement or listing
57 for real property, whether available for rent or sale, that the advertisement includes a digitally
58 altered image. The disclosure shall include a written description of material differences
59 between any digitally altered image and the real property’s actual condition, features, and
60 amenities. The advertisement or listing shall include a publicly accessible and unedited
61 version of any digitally altered image.”.
2

62 Sec. 3. Section 47-2853.197(39) of the District of Columbia Official Code is amended
63 to read as follows:
64 “(39) Knowingly prepared, distributed, or circulated, or caused the preparation,
65 distribution, or circulation of, any false or misleading advertising in connection with the sale,
66 exchange, purchase, lease, or rental of real estate or business, including by violating § 28-
67 3904(nn).”.
