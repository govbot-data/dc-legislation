Dear Secretary Howard:
Today, I am introducing the Dementia Readiness and Awareness in Continuing Education Amendment
Act of 2026, along with Councilmembers Anita Bonds, Charles Allen, Trayon White, Sr., Brianne K.
Nadeau, Janeese Lewis George, Wendell Felder, and Zachary Parker. This legislation would create a one-
time, one-hour continuing education requirement on the diagnosis, treatment, or care of individuals with
Alzheimer’s disease and related dementias (ADRD) for medical professionals who treat adults in a
clinical setting.
Research shows that early and accurate diagnosis of dementia and Alzheimer’s leads to significantly
better outcomes and more supportive and effective care. According to the U.S. Census Bureau, more than
90,000 District residents, roughly 14 percent, are 65 years of age or older.1 The District’s older adult
population is expected to grow substantially in the coming years, making it essential for healthcare
providers to be appropriately trained to address the unique needs of our seniors.
Although there are an estimated 7.4 million Americans living with some form of ADRD, there is a
significant gap in detection, treatment, and care, with only about half of cases being diagnosed.23 Notably,
studies show that Black and Hispanic older adults experience much higher rates of Alzheimer’s than other
groups but are much more likely to be underdiagnosed or diagnosed at late stages.45 According to the
Centers for Disease Control, in 2024, Alzheimer’s was the sixth leading cause of death in America, which
further underscores the need for ADRD education and support.6 To address this, Delaware, Illinois,
Massachusetts, and Rhode Island have mandated that certain licensed medical professionals take ADRD-
related courses as part of their continuing medical education requirements.7 This bill would provide a
similar requirement within the District.

This requirement is intended to provide instruction that will highlight early detection, advances in
research, innovative treatments, and progressing standards of care. The science of Alzheimer’s is
constantly developing, and ADRD detection tools and treatments have evolved significantly in the past
decade. As noted in Alzheimer’s and Dementia, a journal produced by the Alzheimer’s Association, there
are currently 192 active clinical trials assessing 158 drugs in this space.8 Some newer studies and
treatments target plaques in the brain, target tau tangles, brain cell inflammation caused by ADRD, and
the connection between brain health and heart disease; many of these treatments require early intervention
to secure promising results.9 As new research and treatment options emerge, it is vital that we require up-
to-date training for medical professionals to ensure that proper healthcare supports are in place to serve
our senior populations across the District, particularly those most vulnerable to this disease.

35 To amend the District of Columbia Health Occupations Revision Act of 1985 to require a one-
36 time, one-hour course on the diagnosis, treatment, or care of individuals with
37 Alzheimer’s disease and related dementias as part of the continuing medical education
38 requirement for certain medical professionals who treat adult patients in a clinical setting.
39
1

40 BE IT ENACTED BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this
41 act may be cited as the “Dementia Readiness and Awareness in Continuing Education
42 Amendment Act of 2026”.
43 Sec. 2. Section 510(b) of the District of Columbia Health Occupations Revision Act of
44 1985, effective March 25, 1986 (D.C. Law 6-99; D.C. Official Code § 3-1205.10(b)) is amended
45 as follows:
46 (a) Paragraph 4(B)(v) is amended by striking the phrase “; and” and inserting a
47 semicolon in its place.
48 (b) Paragraph 5(B)(vii) is amended by striking the period and inserting “; and” in
49 its place.
50 (c) A new paragraph (6) is added to read as follows:
51 “(6)(A) Require that any individual licensed, registered, or certified to
52 practice in the following health occupations who provides health care services to, and has direct
53 patient interactions with adult patients shall complete, as a one-time requirement, at least one
54 hour of continuing education credit on the diagnosis, treatment, or care of individuals with
55 Alzheimer's disease and related dementias:
56 (i) The practice of medicine;
57 (ii) The practice of physician assistants;
58 (iii) The practice of advanced practice registered nursing;
59 (iv) The practice of registered nursing; and
60 (v) The practice of practical nursing.
61 “(B) An individual who holds an active license, registration, or
62 certification as of the effective date of this act shall complete the requirement described in
2

63 subparagraph (A) of this paragraph by the end of the first renewal period beginning after the
64 effective date of this act.
65 “(C) An individual who receives an initial license, registration, or
66 certification after the effective date of this act shall complete the requirement described in
67 subparagraph (A) of this paragraph by the end of the first renewal period following initial
68 licensure, registration, or certification.
69 “(D) The requirement established in subparagraph (A) of this
70 paragraph may be satisfied by completion of any training that is approved by the boards
71 governing the listed medical practices as meeting the continuing education standards applicable
72 to the individual's health occupation or offered or endorsed by a nationally recognized
73 organization with expertise in Alzheimer's disease and dementia care.
74 “(E) An individual may count the continuing education credit
75 completed under subparagraph (A) of this paragraph toward the total continuing education hours
76 required for renewal of the individual's license, registration, or certification.
77 “(F) The requirement established by subparagraph (A) of this
78 paragraph shall not apply to:
79 “(i) Any licensed health professional who does not see
80 adult patients in a clinical setting in the District; or
81 "(ii) Any licensed health professional who has completed
82 training that meets the requirements under the Dementia Training for Direct Care Workers
83 Support Amendment Act, approved March 16, 2021 (D.C. Law 23-201; D.C. Official Code §7-
84 744.02).”.
