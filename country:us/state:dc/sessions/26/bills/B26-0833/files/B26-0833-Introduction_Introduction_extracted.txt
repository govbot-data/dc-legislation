Please find attached the Health Occupations and Emergency Medical Services Amendment Act of
2026 for enactment by the Council of the District of Columbia.
If enacted, the proposed legislation would amend the District of Columbia Health Occupations
Revision Act of 1985 (HORA), effective March 25, 1986 (D.C. Law 6-99; D.C. Official Code §§
3-1201.01 et seq.), the Health-Care and Community Residence Facility, Hospice, and Home
Care Licensure Act of 1983, and repeal and replace the Emergency Medical Services Act of
2008 to accomplish the following:
Emergency Medical Services (EMS) Modernization: To modernize the District’s EMS system
by creating a Board of Emergency Medical Services to regulate all EMS personnel, including
EMRs, EMTs, AEMTs, Paramedics, Advanced Practice Paramedics, Mobile Integrated
Healthcare Clinicians, EMS Instructors, and Emergency Medical Dispatchers; aligning
Emergency Medical Dispatcher certification and training requirements with national standards;
repealing and replacing the EMS Act of 2008 to establish a modern, efficient, and equitable EMS
system; and clarifying that mobile integrated health agencies are not considered home health care
agencies.
Board Administration and Governance: To strengthen Board administration by granting
subpoena authority to the Mayor and, by delegation, Executive Directors; authorizing the
Director of DC Health to temporarily fill Board vacancies until permanent appointments are
made; clarifying and expanding Board powers to issue guidance, convening subcommittees and
taskforces, and recommend standards and procedures; and updating emeritus licensure
provisions to align with the current HORA framework.
Licensure and Scope of Practice: To update licensure requirements and scopes of practice by
establishing a Certified Community Health Worker license under the Board of Social Work;
creating a temporary waiver process for foreign-licensed health professionals supporting
commercial events; repealing the requirement that the Council review Massage Therapy

regulations; and revising the Board of Medicine’s fee-setting authority to allow adjustments
through rulemaking.
Criminal Background Checks: To modernize criminal background check requirements by
permitting DC Health to receive results directly from the Metropolitan Police Department (MPD)
and the U.S. Department of Justice and eliminating DC Health’s role in collecting background
check fees, improving both efficiency and security.
This legislation strengthens the District’s healthcare infrastructure by modernizing outdated
provisions, improving regulatory clarity, and ensuring a unified approach to licensing, standards,
and safety across the health professions. These updates will support provider readiness, enhance
patient protections, and create a more coordinated and equitable emergency medical services
system for all residents and visitors.
I urge the Council to take prompt and favorable action on the Health Occupations and Emergency
Medical Services Amendment Act of 2026.

Muriel Bowser

18 of Emergency Medical Services to provide for the licensure, regulation, and oversight of

20 instructors; to provide for the certification of community health workers; to add a new Title

24 standards for licensing and certifying the emergency medical services system and
25 operations, including emergency medical services provider entities, vehicles, and training
26 facilities licensed or certified by the Mayor; to provide for a District-wide trauma and
27 emergency care data collection system; to authorize the Mayor to promulgate rules and to

29 violations; and to amend the Health-Care and Community Residence Facility, Hospice, and
30 Home Care Licensure Act of 1983 to provide that mobile integrated health services are not
31 considered home health care agencies; and to repeal the Emergency Medical Services Act
32 of 2008.

402 Sec. 201. Short title.
403 This title may be cited as the “Emergency Medical Services Act of 2026”.
404 Sec. 202. Definitions.
405 For the purposes of this act, the term:
406 (1) “Ambulance” means any privately or publicly owned vehicle specially
407 designed, constructed, modified, or equipped for use as a means for transporting patients in a
408 medical emergency or any privately or publicly owned vehicle that is advertised, marked, or in
409 any way held out as a vehicle for the transportation of patients requiring emergency medical
410 services. The term “ambulance” includes vehicles capable of operating on land, on water, and in
411 air.
412 (2) “Competency evaluation” means any written, oral, or practical examination or
413 assessment of the knowledge or skills of an applicant for certification or re-certification as
414 emergency medical services personnel that is pertinent to the ability of that applicant to perform
415 their duties. Each competency evaluation may include one or more sub-parts, including
416 individual demonstrations and assessments of the applicant's performance in actual or simulated
417 emergency situations.
418 (3) “Emergency” means the existence of circumstances in which the element of
419 time in treating, or in transporting to medical treatment, a person who is ill, injured, wounded, or
420 otherwise incapacitated is essential to the health or life of that person, and in which rescue
421 operations, or competent first aid, or both may be essential to the health or life of that person.
422 (4)(A) “Emergency medical response vehicle” means a vehicle or conveyance of
423 which the primary intended use is to respond to the scene of a medical emergency for the
18

424 purpose of rendering medical assistance, including the provision of medical assistance on the
425 scene or the transportation of patients to a healthcare facility or other treatment facility.
426 (B) The term “emergency medical response vehicle” includes:
427 (i) Ambulances that operate as motor vehicles, watercraft, or
428 aircraft; and
429 (ii) Fire engines, motor vehicles, utility vehicles, bicycles, and
430 other motorized or non-motorized ground, water, or air vehicles used to transport emergency
431 medical services personnel, supplies, or equipment to the scene of an emergency.
432 (C) The term “emergency medical response vehicle” does not include any
433 vehicle that responds to the scene of a medical emergency that is not intended to render medical
434 assistance, such as a law enforcement vehicle.
435 (5) “Emergency medical services agency” means an entity engaged in the
436 business or service of one or more of the following:
437 (A) Responding to requests for emergency medical assistance;
438 (B) Transporting patients from the scene of an emergency to a healthcare
439 facility or other treatment facility;
440 (C) Providing medical assistance to patients on the scene of an emergency
441 or in transit from the scene of an emergency to a healthcare facility or other treatment facility;
442 (D) Providing medical assistance to patients at special events;
443 (E) Providing medical care through mobile integrated healthcare services;
444 or
445 (F) Providing non-emergency patient transport.
19

446 (6) “Emergency medical services training facility” means an institution or entity
447 engaged in the business or service of providing one or more courses of study or training designed
448 to prepare interested persons for the oral, written, or practical examinations required for licensure
449 or renewal as emergency medical services clinicians.
450 (7) “Healthcare facility” means a hospital, maternity center, ambulatory surgical
451 facility, or hospice, as defined in section 2 of the Health-Care and Community Residence
452 Facility, Hospice and Home Care Licensure Act of 1983, effective February 24, 1984 (D.C. Law
453 5-48; D.C. Official Code § 44-501) or any other facility designated by the Director of the
454 Department of Health for the purpose of rendering medical, clinical, or supportive care.
455 (8) “Medical control system” means a system wherein direct clinical instructions
456 are given to emergency medical services clinicians in the field by authorized healthcare
457 professionals with independent practice authority.
458 (9) “Medical director” means a physician licensed to practice medicine in the
459 District who has responsibility for the medical oversight of the clinical operations of an
460 emergency medical services agency.
461 (10) “Mobile integrated health services” means services provided by an
462 emergency medical services agency through the use of licensed clinicians to patients who do not
463 require emergency medical transportation. Mobile integrated healthcare services may include:
464 (A) Preventive and primary care services;
465 (B) Chronic disease monitoring and management;
466 (C) Post-discharge follow-up;
467 (D) Behavioral health support and crisis intervention;
468 (E) Social determinants of health screenings and referrals;
20

469 (F) Non-emergent care coordination and patient navigation services; and
470 (G) Health education and public health outreach.
471 (11) “Mutual aid” means an agreement whereby the District of Columbia requests
472 the assistance of an outside entity to provide supplemental or specialized emergency medical
473 services in an emergent situation, pursuant to established protocols.
474 (12) “Non-emergency” means a situation in which a patient or person requires
475 medical monitoring that does not meet the time-sensitive nature of an emergency.
476 (13) “Patient” means an individual for whom an emergency medical services
477 agency or clinician is activated or engaged to provide emergency medical services when the
478 individual’s condition requires medical observation, monitoring, assessment, or treatment for an
479 acute or chronic illness, disease, injury, or other disability.
480 (14) “Qualified healthcare professional” means a person licensed and qualified in
481 accordance with the District of Columbia Health Occupations Revision Act of 1985, effective
482 March 25, 1986 (D.C. Law 6-99; D.C. Official Code § 3-1201.01 et seq.), and with rules
483 promulgated pursuant to that act, to engage in the act of prescribing and of rendering medical
484 care and advice under the circumstances presented.
485 (14) “Special event emergency medical services” means the organized and
486 coordinated provision of emergency medical care, medical response, and support of public health
487 at planned events, gatherings, or activities where attendance, participation, venue characteristics,
488 or general event operations create increased risk or potential for illness or injury.
489 (15) “Specialty systems of care” means coordinated, Districtwide clinical care
490 frameworks organized around specific time sensitive or resource intensive medical conditions, in
491 which designated hospitals and supporting partners meet defined standards for capability,
21

492 capacity, expertise, and data reporting as defined by the Mayor. Specialty systems of care
493 include trauma, stroke, cardiac, burn, and pediatric care systems.
494 (16) “Stretcher van agency” means an entity engaged in the business or service of
495 transporting patients who require the use of a stretcher and do not require medical monitoring or
496 treatment during transport.
497 (17) “Stretcher van vehicle” means a vehicle or conveyance used to transport non-
498 emergency passengers who require the use of a stretcher and does not provide medical
499 monitoring or treatment during transport.
500 (18) “Treatment facility” means an urgent care center, treatment center, clinic, or
501 other facility or office at which medical or psychological services are performed, and which has
502 been designated by the Mayor as a facility authorized to receive patients transported by
503 emergency medical services agencies or clinicians.
504 Sec. 203. Applicability and exemptions.

508 medical services agencies and owners and operators of emergency medical response vehicles;
509 (2) Entity or individual providing non-emergency transport services within the
510 District, whether public or private or for-profit or not-for-profit, including owners and operators
511 of stretcher van agencies and owners and operators of stretcher van vehicles; and
512 (3) Entity or individual providing emergency medical services training and
513 instruction, whether public or private or for-profit or not-for-profit, within the District, including
514 owners and operators.

Conclusion
Funds are sufficient in the fiscal year 2026 through fiscal year 2029 budget and financial plan to
implement the bill.
Background
The bill updates1 how Emergency Medical Services (EMS) professionals practicing in the District of
Columbia are licensed and regulated. The bill establishes a nine-member Emergency Medical
Services Board (Board) to regulate EMS clinicians and educators within the District of Columbia. The
Board will have regulatory authority over Emergency Medical Responders, Emergency Medical
Technicians, Advanced Emergency Medical Technicians, Paramedics, Advanced Practice Paramedics,
Mobile Integrated Healthcare Clinicians, Emergency Medical Services Instructors, and Emergency
Medical Dispatchers. The Board will be supported by a full-time Executive Director, who will
implement and administer the Board's orders. The bill expands2 the types of EMS agencies regulated
by DC Health and transitions the regulation of EMS professionals to the new Board.

The bill allows3 the Director of DC Health to appoint a temporary member to boards when vacancies
on a board are equal to or more than 50 percent of the total number of members. Temporary
members must meet all qualifications required of members and cannot be appointed for more than
120 days. Temporary members cannot be appointed more than once to a specific board as a
temporary member.
The bill establishes a license and scope of practice for Certified Community Health Workers and Oral
and Maxillofacial Surgeons.
Finally, the bill clarifies4 that licensed mobile integrated health agencies are not considered home
health agencies under the law. Mobile integrated health agencies bring healthcare directly to
patients' homes for services such as follow-up care, chronic disease management, and connecting
individuals to social services.
Financial Plan Impact
Funds are sufficient in the fiscal year 2026 through fiscal year 2029 budget and financial plan to
implement the bill. DC Health can establish the new Board, regulate EMS agencies, appoint temporary
board members, and establish new licenses with existing staff and resources. There is no cost to
clarifying the definition of home health agencies.
