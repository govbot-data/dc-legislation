Today, along with Councilmember Christina Henderson, I am introducing the “Expanding Access for Student
Identification Empowers Readers (EASIER) Act of 2026”.
This legislation builds upon legislation introduced by Councilmember Henderson to increase the utility of school-
issued student identification (ID) cards. In October 2025, Councilmember Henderson introduced B26-0240, the
“Student Identification Card Act of 2025”, to require each local education agency (LEA) to issue ID cards to
middle school and high school students. That legislation is now pending before the Committee of the Whole.
The EASIER Act requires each ID issued by a local education agency under the Student Identification Card Act to
include all the functionality of a District of Columbia Public Library (DCPL) card, enable access to Department of
Parks and Recreation (DPR) facilities, convey how to contact the national Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, and be
provided free of charge to students.
Before September 2025, the District issued students DC ONE cards, consolidated credentials that served as de
facto student IDs, library cards, and DPR access cards while providing several other functions. Since the
September 2025 sunsetting of the ONE card program, 11 out of 22 District of Columbia Public Schools (DCPS)
high schools and approximately 25 District charter schools have opted to continue to provide student IDs, paying
for them out of their own budgets. Other schools, however, have gone without them.
While the Student Identification Card Act of 2025 restores student IDs to all District middle and high schoolers,
this legislation takes another step toward restoring the critical functionality of the DC ONE card. It adopts a
commonsense approach to integrated credentialing with the potential to greatly increase youth access to library
resources. Other jurisdictions, such as Chicago, Nashville, and Des Moines, have enacted similar programs with
their library systems, seeing large increases in library access among economically disadvantaged students and
English language learners.1 Currently, DCPS students are able to access some of DCPL’s resources, like checking
out or placing books on hold, by giving a librarian their unique DCPS ID number. Relying on this practice,
however, assumes children and teens have this number memorized or written down for out of school use.

Children who are not enrolled in DCPS can only get a DCPL library card if they are accompanied by a parent or
guardian who presents proof of residence and identity. Not every parent has the time or ability to do this. What’s
more, the information needed to obtain a card is already submitted during the school enrollment process.
Before the sunsetting of the ONE card, all District resident students were able to use their ONE cards as their
library cards, eliminating a barrier to use and conveniently keeping their access to resources on a single
credential. With this legislation, the District can open up thousands of new library accounts for its middle and
high school students across all LEAs, empowering them to access a lifetime of learning resources and a passion
for reading.
Similarly, there is no uniform process for verifying the identity of a young person entering a DPR recreation
center or pool. Student ID cards with this function built in would eliminate redundant and inconsistent processes
that make it more difficult for young people to take advantage of all that the city has to offer, whether it be
enrichment activities or sports outside of school hours.
Finally, 25 states have enacted laws requiring the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline to be listed on student IDs for
public middle and high school students to improve access to mental health services and awareness of this
lifesaving resource.2 The inclusion of the Lifeline number on each card would bring the District of Columbia in
line with this national best practice regarding youth suicide prevention.
I look forward to working with my colleagues and the Executive to provide these valuable resources to our
students in a convenient format once more.
Should you have any questions about this legislation, please contact either Emelie Shany, Legislative Assistant
at eshany@dccouncil.gov or Nora Charles, Legislative Assistant at ncharles@dccouncil.gov.
