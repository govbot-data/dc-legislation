Dear Chairman Mendelson:
Enclosed for consideration by the Council of the District of Columbia is the “Banking Code
Modernization Amendment Act of 2026” (“Bill”). The purpose of the Bill is to repeal the Money
Transmitters Act of 2000 and replace it with updated standards reflective of technological and
industry changes while enhancing consumer protection safeguards. It will also amend the
Securities Act of 2000 to create mandatory reporting requirements for suspected financial
exploitation of vulnerable adults and update technical aspects of the student loan servicing laws
and appraisal management laws.
The updated money transmitters sections of the Bill are based on a Conference of State Bank
Supervisors model act that has been adopted by several states, but this version was modified to
include common licensing and regulatory standards that will add efficiencies and enhance
consumer protection. The Bill also includes updated definitions applicable to money transmitters
and clarifies the licensing process. Further, the Bill adds safety and soundness requirements
including net worth, surety bond, and permissible investments. Additionally, the Bill enables
multistate licensing and multistate supervision and clarifies that virtual currency is included in
money transmission.
I urge the Council to take prompt and favorable action on the Bill. If you have any questions,
please have your staff contact Samuel Fuller, Associate Commissioner for Banking at the D.C.
Department of Insurance, Securities and Banking at 202-442-7839.
Sincerely,
Muriel Bowser

40 TITLE I. FINANCIAL EXPLOITATION OF OLDER AND VULNERABLE
41 ADULTS.
42 Sec. 101. Short title.
43 This act may be cited as the “Senior Safe Act of 2026”.
44 Sec. 102. Definitions.
45 (1) “Adult Protective Services” or “APS” means Adult Protective Services
46 as overseen by the District of Columbia Department of Aging and Community Living or
47 its successor.
48 (2) “Depository financial institutions” means District of Columbia-
49 chartered banks, state-chartered banks, federally chartered banks, District of Columbia-
50 chartered credit unions, state-chartered credit unions, federally chartered credit unions,
51 trust companies operating in the District, and any other financial institution, regardless of
52 chartering authority, to a District of Columbia resident.
53 (3) “Department” means the Department of Insurance, Securities &
54 Banking.
55 (4) “Eligible adult” means:
56 (A) A person 65 years of age or older; or
57 (B) An individual 18 years of age or older who:
58 (i) Is highly vulnerable to abuse, neglect, self-neglect, or
59 exploitation because of a physical or mental impairment, self-neglect, or incapacity; or
60 (ii) Has recently been or is being abused, neglected, or
61 exploited by another or meets the criteria for self-neglect.”.
2

62 (5) “Financial exploitation” means the illegal, unauthorized, or fraudulent
63 use, or deprivation of use of the money, assets, or property of an elderly person or
64 vulnerable adult with the intention of benefiting someone other than the elderly person or
65 vulnerable adult.
66 (6) “Person reasonably associated with the eligible adult" means:
67 (A) An authorized contact provided by an eligible adult to a
68 depository financial institution;
69 (B) A fiduciary authorized to manage some or all of the financial
70 affairs of an eligible adult;
71 (C) A co-owner, additional authorized signatory or beneficiary, or
72 other person authorized to transact business on the eligible adult’s account;
73 (D) An attorney or financial professional known to represent or
74 have represented with respect to, or to assist in the management of, the financial affairs of
75 the eligible adult;
76 (E) A parent, spouse, adult child, sibling or other known family
77 member or close associate of an eligible adult; or
78 (F) A person identified by the Department as associated with the
79 eligible adult.
80 Sec 103. Scope.
81 All depository financial institutions that operate in the District of Columbia shall
82 have the authority to exercise the actions related to financial exploitation under this Act.
3

83 Sec. 104. Refusal to engage in or execute a transaction, delay transaction, or
84 refusal to disburse funds.
85 (a) All depository financial institutions may refuse to engage in or execute a
86 transaction, may delay a transaction, or may refuse to disburse funds if any employee or
87 agent of the institution:
88 (1) Believes in good faith that the transaction or disbursement may
89 involve, facilitate, result in, or contribute to the financial exploitation of an eligible adult
90 and believes in good faith that the person is an eligible adult; or
91 (2) Makes or has actual knowledge that another person has made, a report
92 to the Department or the APS hotline of the Department of Aging and Community
93 Living, stating a good faith belief that the transaction or disbursement may involve,
94 facilitate, result in, or contribute to the financial exploitation of an eligible adult.
95 (b) A depository financial institution may continue to refuse to engage in or
96 execute a transaction, delay a transaction, or refuse to disburse funds for a period no
97 longer than 30 business days after the date upon which such transaction or disbursement
98 was initially requested based on a good faith belief that the transaction or disbursement
99 may involve, facilitate, result in, or contribute to the financial exploitation of an eligible
100 adult unless otherwise ordered by a court of competent jurisdiction.
101 (c) A depository financial institution may extend the expiration date of its initial
102 refusal or delay by an additional 30 business days if:
103 (1) A depository financial institution, APS, a law enforcement agency, or
104 the Department makes a request for a court order to block or extend the refused
105 disbursement or transaction, or
4

106 (2) APS, a law enforcement agency, or the Department requests that the
107 depository financial institution extend for a specified number of business days the
108 expiration date of its refusal to engage in or execute a transaction, delay of a transaction,
109 or refusal to disburse funds.
110 Sec. 105. Reporting.
111 (a) Upon refusing to engage in or execute a transaction, delaying a transaction, or
112 refusing to disburse funds, the depository financial institution shall report such refusal or
113 delay within 10 business days to the Department and APS (collectively “the agencies”).
114 (b) The depository financial institution may report any information or records
115 relevant to a report or investigation to the agencies and to a court-appointed guardian ad
116 litem for the eligible adult who is the subject of the investigation.
117 Sec. 106. Assistance by persons reasonably associated.
118 (a) The depository financial institution may provide information regarding the
119 suspected financial exploitation to a person reasonably associated with the eligible adult
120 sufficient to enable the person to consult with or assist the eligible adult in avoiding or
121 remedying the suspected financial exploitation, or exercise any fiduciary powers it
122 possesses to protect the eligible adult, unless the financial institution has reason to believe
123 the person is knowingly engaged in or facilitating the financial exploitation of the eligible
124 adult.
125 Sec. 107. Immunity.
126 (a) Absent gross negligence or willful misconduct, the depository financial
127 institution and its staff shall be immune from administrative, civil, or criminal liability
128 under District law for:
5

129 (1) Providing information or records to the agencies, to a court-appointed
130 guardian ad litem, or a party reasonably associated with the eligible adult, or
131 (2) Refusing to engage in or execute a transaction, delaying a transaction,
132 or refusing to disburse funds pursuant to this subsection.
133 (b) The authority of a depository financial institution to refuse to engage in or
134 execute a transaction, to delay a transaction, or to refuse to disburse funds pursuant to this
135 Act shall not be contingent upon whether the depository financial institution has reported
136 suspected financial exploitation of an eligible adult.
137 Sec. 108. Rulemaking authority.
138 The Mayor may adopt rules and regulations to implement the provisions of this
139 act.
