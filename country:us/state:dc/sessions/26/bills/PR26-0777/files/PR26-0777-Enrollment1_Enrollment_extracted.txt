ENROLLED ORIGINAL
A RESOLUTION
26-494
IN THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA
July 14, 2026
To declare the existence of an emergency with respect to the need to amend the Homeless
Services Reform Act of 2005 to clarify the authorization to share certain data within the
Department of Human Services and between the Department of Human Services and
other District agencies providing health or human services to facilitate the determination
of eligibility for federal and District health and human service programs and benefits, to
enhance the coordination of such programs and benefits, and for other purposes.
RESOLVED, BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this
resolution may be cited as the “Health and Human Service Programs and Benefits Eligibility and
Delivery Data-Sharing Clarifications Emergency Declaration Resolution of 2026”.
Sec. 2. (a) There exists an immediate need to enact legislation to amend the Homeless
Services Reform Act of 2005 (“HSRA”), effective October 22, 2005 (D.C. Law 16-35; D.C.
Official Code § 4-751.01 et seq.).
(b) The District’s long-standing waiver of the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance
Program’s (“SNAP”) time limits for Able-Bodied Adults Without Dependents (“ABAWD”)
expired at the end of calendar year 2025. The District was unable to renew its ABAWD Waiver
due to changes in federal law. As a result, beginning June 2026, whenever an ABAWD applies
for or seeks to renew their SNAP eligibility, they must be screened to establish either that they
comply with, or are exempt from, the ABAWD work requirements. After screening, non-
compliant, non-exempt ABAWDs may only receive SNAP benefits for up to 3 months in a set
36-month time period.
(c) The Department of Human Services (“DHS”) Economic Security Administration
(“ESA”) is attempting to lessen the burden on SNAP ABAWD customers by using data matches
to proactively identify time limit exemptions for which ABAWDs may qualify. Information
maintained by the DHS Family Services Administration (“FSA”) in connection with programs it
administers that are governed by the HSRA could be used by ESA to determine that certain
ABAWDs receiving services from both DHS administrations qualify for exemptions from the
SNAP ABAWD time limits.
(d) ESA began screening ABAWDs this month. Emergency legislation is required to
clarify that, pursuant to the requirements and procedures of Title I of the Data-Sharing and
Information Coordination Amendment Act of 2010, effective December 4, 2010 (D.C. Law 18-

273; D.C. Official Code § 7-241 et seq.), ESA may leverage FSA data to facilitate ABAWD time
limit exemption determinations for FSA homeless services customers who also receive SNAP. If
ESA cannot determine that an ABAWD customer is exempt, the customer will begin to accrue
countable months towards the 3-month time limit after their screening. Looking to the future, this
same FSA data could also facilitate exempting customers from new Medicaid Community
Engagement requirements set to go into effect in 2027.
Sec. 3. The Council of the District of Columbia determines that the circumstances
enumerated in section 2 constitute emergency circumstances making it necessary that the Health
and Human Service Programs and Benefits Eligibility and Delivery Data-Sharing Clarifications
Emergency Amendment Act of 2026 be adopted after a single reading.
Sec. 4. This resolution shall take effect immediately.
